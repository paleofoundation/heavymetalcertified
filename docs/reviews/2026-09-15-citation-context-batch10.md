## Evidence and editorial findings

- Tea leaf concentrations, measured infusions and modeled transfer are distinct.
  The Chinese-tea cadmium transfer is 9.41%, not the zinc value 46.3%. Some
  abstracts cannot verify detailed leaf-age or brewing-multiplier claims.
- Climate models differ in compartments and pathways. Wang's outputs concern
  seawater and lower food-web organisms, not the article's commercial-fish
  forecast. Toxicological intake benchmarks are not occurrence baselines tied
  to a particular climate year.
- Cookware results require the measured medium and preparation basis. Sultan
  measured filtered cooking liquid in the meat arm; Mahdi's methods do not
  establish the tightly controlled causal design claimed in the article.
  Riyadh's narrative conflicts with its tables. Rice results use dried material,
  and the cited cooked value describes one condition rather than a pooled mean.
- Nickel occurrence does not isolate a pollution-free physiological baseline.
  China's cited nickel limit concerns hydrogenated oils rather than beans.
  EU limits now exist; the current HMI synthesis itself corrects earlier claims.
- Rice irrigation has context-dependent arsenic/cadmium trade-offs. The original
  EU rice cadmium entry is 0.15 mg/kg, not 0.10. The studies' publication order
  differs from their experimental chronology. FDA explicitly recognizes that
  reducing one contaminant can increase another.
