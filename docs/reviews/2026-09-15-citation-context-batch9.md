## Evidence and editorial findings

- Selenium ratios and proposed indices do not independently relax a legal
  mercury ceiling or demonstrate a protective effect for every fish. Dietary
  intake benchmarks are distinct from product concentrations.
- FDA advice about cooking remains relevant, but BfR's pooled-food design does
  not provide the article's claimed matched fresh/cooked experiment. Quito
  measured total mercury, not a directly measured stable methylmercury fraction.
- Hong Kong's grace provision applies to any food meeting either complete
  regime. China's future-tense regulatory framing is stale. Taiwan's cited
  method has a specific scope. GSO catalogue access establishes identity and
  scope, not the inaccessible complete numerical schedule.
- The Indian amendment was checked against its exact Gazette. MERCOSUR's cited
  minutes establish a consultation at that time, not its latest outcome.
  FDA formula monitoring results are not new contaminant limits.
- The Cowell spice paper contains 32 samples, not 127, and uses a candy-based
  comparison. The MMWR's case count, publication year and applesauce-versus-spice
  concentrations need distinct labels. A general adulteration rate is not a
  lead-adulteration prevalence estimate.
- The complete SCF tin opinion does not derive the asserted 200/100/50 maxima
  or establish an infant no-effect level. Those legal limits have separate
  product exclusions. The five-person historical trial had an inconsistent
  repeat response; its faecal recovery finding concerns rats. Canned-fruit
  surveillance did not report the tin-dose model attributed to it.

Access and edition limits are retained in each record. HMI preprints remain
interpretations rather than independent experiments. Article prose is unchanged.
