## Evidence and editorial findings

- Water, workplace-air and food rules regulate different quantities. ECHA's
  cited chromium restriction notice is a proposal; local implementation and
  dated guidance matter.
- Mixture models, arsenic margins of exposure and cadmium intake fractions
  are different metrics. The Finnish sample and JECFA's two 96% scenarios
  need correct attribution; models do not establish observed injury.
- The claimed Bao bivalve study has a mismatched DOI pointing to a different
  gold-nanoparticle validation paper. Reference 1 remains unresolved, with
  the attempted identification recorded separately. Other bivalve studies
  differ in analyte, tissue and wet/dry basis.
- Organoarsenical experiments include simulated digestion, an exposed model
  alga and cross-sectional serum associations. They do not establish human
  absorption, commercial-product prevalence or arsenobetaine causation.
- Breastmilk papers have observational, analytical and reporting limitations.
  Postpartum milk can be a prenatal-exposure biomarker; it cannot establish
  that subsequent feeding caused birth weight. FDA explicitly regulates
  donor-milk products as food, qualifying universal exclusion claims.
- Botanical pharmacopoeia comparisons describe written standards rather than
  enforcement. Case reports do not measure prevalence or manufacturer intent.
  The historical API comparator was checked with edition and access limits;
  distinct FDA/CFR sources document relevant controls.

Source drawers and evidence notes carry these distinctions. Article body prose
was not rewritten. Shared-author HMI syntheses retain explicit preprint status.
