## Evidence and editorial findings

- Organic regulations explicitly address heavy-metal contamination and residue
  testing. They do not provide a universal finished-product concentration
  certificate. Claims that organic rules are wholly silent are too broad.
- The Dearing study's examined version awaits peer review. Lead and arsenic were
  excluded from comparative statistics because of few detections, so it does not
  establish no organic effect on those metals.
- The cocoa paper review is limited to the authors' accessible abstract and
  bibliographic record. It does not establish an organic comparison.
- The rice risk assessment and later nonbinding action-level guidance are
  distinct documents with different dates and purposes.
- The Promix monetary example matches a private KASB settlement, not the
  bibliography's unverified People v. Promix consent judgment. The drawer makes
  that mismatch explicit and does not claim liability was established.
- Marketing books provide conceptual arguments, not forecasts for HMTc.
- The reused FDA detection-rate, industry-survey authorship, superseded-manual
  and modeled-benefit limitations remain documented in each record's evidence.

Page prose and bibliography wording remain unchanged. Each reviewed record keeps
