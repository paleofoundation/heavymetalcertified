## Evidence and editorial findings

- Q3D is a risk-based pharmaceutical framework with route-specific exposure
  limits, not a universal non-detect rule. The anabolic-steroid study did not
  test peptides or SARMs. Historical USP editions and abstract-only access
  are identified. The unavailable FDA 2017 announcement remains unresolved
  in both briefings rather than being replaced with a later warning.
- Aquaculture comparisons include observational and modeled data, species
  differences and estimated methylmercury fractions. Certification scheme
  traceability clauses do not establish a common contaminant-testing rule.
- Formula studies differ in sampling period, preparation basis and product
  class; their results do not establish a universal powder-versus-liquid
  ranking, absorbed-dose comparison or causal processing effect.
- EU arsenic and nickel rules distinguish adoption, entry into force,
  application and existing-stock provisions. The HMI summaries are secondary
  records, including a mislabeled nickel effective date.
- The historical EU acts contradict a universally tightening timeline:
  Regulation 488/2014 explicitly raised some cadmium limits. The 2001 act
  applied in 2002; 2023/915 incorporated mercury, whose fish categories have
  three tiers. The source drawers preserve these qualifications without
  rewriting surrounding article prose.
