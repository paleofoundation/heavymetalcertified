## Evidence and editorial findings

- US Baby Food Laws references 4 and 6 remain basic drawers. The exact Vermont
  enacted text could not be retrieved reliably; the cited HMI article returned
  404. Both are recorded in the unresolved queue without substitute summaries.
- Virginia excludes infant formula. Illinois's enacted sales provision differs
  from the introduced bill, and testing and disclosure have different dates.
- Packaging metal limits apply to packaging, not food concentrations or migration.
  Some amendments have category-specific exceptions and transition dates.
- The chocolate paper's cadmium organic comparison uses 37 observations. The
  lower later mean does not demonstrate that enforcement caused the difference.
- Fertilizer limits depend on jurisdiction, product category, application rate
  and concentration basis. China's official catalog verifies the standard's
  identity, not its unavailable numerical table. EU review dates do not enact
  an automatic lower cadmium limit.
- The wastewater studies use dried samples and modeled health indices. Unit and
  comparison-basis inconsistencies prevent treating every reported exceedance
  as an independently verified food-law breach. Historical soil causation is
  not established by contemporaneous cross-sectional sampling.
- GB 2762-2022 has been replaced by GB 2762-2025. The FSANZ infant-formula
  amendment is Australia-specific, with a transition period. Nickel form,
  chromium speciation, and the version of the HMTc manual materially affect
  interpretation of the Four vs Eight references.
