# askFSIS Policy Inquiry — HMTc mark on FSIS-regulated products

**Status:** Draft for Karen's review before submission. Internal — not published.
**Channel:** askFSIS (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/contact-us/askfsis), category *Labeling*.
**Purpose:** Pre-clear the Heavy Metal Tested & Certified seal wording for meat, poultry, and egg products, and confirm the substantiation the agency expects, before we release seal artwork to establishments that ship FSIS-regulated product.
**Owner:** Institute of Contaminant Standards (operator of the HMTc program), Paleo Certified, Inc.

---

## Why we are asking (internal note, do not send)

A third-party certification is a "special statement or claim" under 9 CFR 412.1, so any establishment placing our seal on a regulated label must obtain sketch approval through LSAS, and the label will be judged on its face for whether it is truthful, substantiated, and not misleading (9 CFR 317.8 / 381.129). We cannot obtain a blanket certifier pre-approval — FSIS approves labels, not programs. But an askFSIS policy response on the *wording and substantiation* of the seal is citable, reduces per-label rejection risk for our licensees, and converts "our name reads as a measurement, not a purity claim" into an agency-stated position. This inquiry seeks that.

Do **not** frame the seal as "clean," "pure," "toxin-free," or "safe" anywhere in the inquiry. Frame it consistently as a *tested measurement against published maximum levels.*

---

## Inquiry text (send this)

**Subject:** Acceptability and substantiation of a third-party heavy-metal certification mark on FSIS-regulated labels

We operate Heavy Metal Tested & Certified (HMTc), a third-party certification program that certifies finished consumer products, including meat, poultry, and egg products, against published maximum concentrations for heavy metals. We are writing under the Labeling category to confirm the agency's expectations before we provide seal artwork to establishments.

The certification mark asserts a single, defined claim: that the certified product was tested at or below every published HMTc maximum level for its subcategory, on a ten-analyte panel (lead, total arsenic, cadmium, methylmercury, total mercury, inorganic arsenic, nickel, aluminum, hexavalent chromium, and tin), in the product's native basis. The maximum levels are published in advance at heavymetalcertified.com/standards, each traceable to the strictest maximum level set by a credible government regulator for that product and analyte, converted to the product's native basis. The mark is displayed with a required scope line stating what was measured and to what limit, and with a public verification address at which the certificate and the governing standard can be confirmed.

We would be grateful for the agency's guidance on three points:

1. **Sketch approval.** We understand that a label bearing this mark is not eligible for generic approval and must be submitted as a sketch label through LSAS for evaluation as a special claim under 9 CFR 412.1. Is that understanding correct for a third-party certification of this kind?

2. **Substantiation on file.** We intend to instruct each establishment to retain, and to make available on request during inspection, the following in support of the claim: (a) the current HMTc certificate identifying the product, facility, and term; (b) the published HMTc subcategory standard the mark refers to, stating the maximum level and basis; (c) the accredited-laboratory analytical report for the certified lot; and (d) the trademark license authorizing display of the mark. Does this documentation package meet the agency's expectation for substantiating a special claim of this type, and is there anything the agency would additionally require?

3. **Wording.** The mark's text reads "Heavy Metal Tested & Certified," accompanied by the scope line and verification address described above. It makes no representation that the product is "clean," "pure," "free of" heavy metals, or "safe." Does the agency see any element of this wording that it would consider unsupportable or potentially misleading on a meat, poultry, or egg label, and is there wording the agency would require us to add — for example, a definition of the claim or a required qualifier — for the mark to be acceptable on its face?

We are happy to provide the seal artwork, a specimen scope line, and the published standard for any product category the agency would like to see. Thank you.
