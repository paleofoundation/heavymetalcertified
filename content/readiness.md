Private Readiness is the controlled first stage for a brand that wants to understand its position before entering a formal certification decision.

It is not certification. It creates no certificate, no public applicant status, and no right to display the mark. Its purpose is to identify the applicable standard, determine whether current evidence can be reused, expose genuine gaps, and give the brand a rational next decision.

## The six-question starting point

A useful readiness process should begin with only the information needed to determine scope:

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What product category and subcategory apply? | The standard is product-form specific rather than brand-wide. |
| Who is the intended consumer? | Infant, child, and general-population products may fall under different requirements. |
| In what form is the product sold? | Powder, ready-to-feed, dry-as-sold, and as-consumed bases are not interchangeable. |
| How many SKUs and distinct formulations are involved? | Shared formulas, facilities, suppliers, and flavor systems can be sequenced together. |
| Which analytes and lots have already been tested? | Qualified evidence may reduce duplication; incomplete evidence still informs readiness. |
| Is the brand seeking readiness only or a formal certification decision? | The data handling, sample plan, cost, and public consequences differ. |

The output should be immediate enough to be useful: applicable standard status, known evidence requirements, likely gaps, preliminary timeline, principal cost drivers, and the appropriate next route.

## Show us what you already know

A mature brand should not be treated as though it has done nothing. Existing laboratory reports, supplier COAs, historical lot data, retailer testing, and internal specifications should be reviewed against published acceptance criteria.

### Evidence Reuse Passport

| Criterion | Acceptance question |
|---|---|
| Laboratory | Was the laboratory appropriately accredited for the relevant method and matrix? |
| Analyte and species | Was the required chemical form measured, rather than an analytically different total or surrogate? |
| Method | Is the method compatible with the certification protocol and product matrix? |
| Sensitivity | Are the reporting limit and quantitation limit sufficiently low for the applicable standard? |
| Sample identity | Is the result tied to a named product, product form, lot, and production run? |
| Recency | Is the result recent enough to describe the current formula, suppliers, process, and facility? |
| Chain of custody | Can the sample's identity and handling be substantiated? |
| Reporting basis | Are units, moisture basis, preparation basis, and product form clear and validly convertible? |
| Completeness | Are all required analytes, reflex tests, metadata, and uncertainty fields available? |

Each record should receive one of four outcomes:

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Accepted | The evidence can satisfy the identified certification requirement. |
| Accepted with qualification | The evidence can be used for a defined part of the requirement, with the qualification recorded. |
| Readiness evidence only | The result is useful for risk assessment or planning but cannot support a certification decision. |
| New testing required | A material criterion is missing or incompatible. |

The operating principle is simple: test what is genuinely missing, but do not lower the evidence standard merely because a test has already been purchased.

## What the brand receives

Private Readiness should produce a decision packet rather than a sales call summary.

| Output | Decision supported |
|---|---|
| Product and standard map | Which exact standard and product basis apply to each SKU? |
| Evidence inventory | Which records were reviewed and how were they classified? |
| Gap map | Which analytes, lots, methods, metadata, or controls remain missing? |
| Portfolio sequence | Which products are likely quick certifications, which need additional evidence, and which need sourcing or formulation work? |
| Cost architecture | Which cost components are expected and which variables can change them? |
| Timing architecture | Which events sit on the critical path and which dependencies can delay it? |
| Risk flags | Are there analytical, regulatory, supplier, formulation, facility, or data-integrity concerns requiring attention before formal certification? |
| Recommended route | Proceed, test a defined gap, enter confidential remediation, monitor, reformulate, change source, or defer. |

## Portfolio triage

A portfolio should not be treated as one indivisible application. Products can be grouped by formula, ingredient base, facility, supplier structure, flavor addition, age group, and standard.

| Readiness band | Interpretation | Typical action |
|---|---|---|
| Ready | Qualified evidence and production lots appear sufficient for formal certification | Confirm scope and enter the certification baseline. |
| Evidence gap | Product appears suitable but one or more analytes, lots, methods, or records are missing | Test the defined gap or complete the missing record. |
| Intervention likely | Supplier, ingredient, process, packaging, or formulation risk is material | Investigate and correct before formal certification. |
| Escalation required | A possible legal exceedance, data-integrity concern, or serious control failure exists | Stop the readiness workflow and obtain appropriate quality, regulatory, and legal review. |
| Insufficient information | The current record cannot support a responsible conclusion | Collect the minimum missing information before spending on a full program. |

## Confidentiality and its limits

The program should not publish a list of Private Readiness participants or describe a readiness outcome as a public certification failure. Readiness materials should be handled as confidential business information under the applicable agreement and access controls.

That commercial confidentiality is not legal privilege. It does not override a recall, reporting duty, subpoena, legal hold, regulator request, retailer contract, or other obligation that applies to the brand or the program. The brand remains responsible for obtaining its own legal advice and complying with its own duties.

Private readiness data must not be transferred into the public Heavy Metal Index. HMI uses public and separately governed evidence sources; it is not a repository for applicant files.

## Moving into certification

The brand chooses whether and when to proceed. Formal certification begins only after the scope, applicable standard, evidence plan, cost treatment, sample plan, and consequence rules are understood.
