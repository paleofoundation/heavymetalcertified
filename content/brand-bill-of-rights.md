Subject to applicable law and the executed program agreements, Heavy Metal Tested &amp; Certified operates to the following commitments.

## The rights

| Right | Program commitment |
|---|---|
| 1. Know the standard | The applicable product form, standard, edition, analytes, basis, and decision rules are identifiable before formal testing. Limits are published before anything is certified against them. |
| 2. Know the complete expected cost | The written quote identifies program, laboratory, logistics, confirmation, remediation, surveillance, renewal, rush, and optional cost treatment before samples are submitted — and states which party pays the laboratory. |
| 3. Know the possible consequences | The consequence classes, confirmation route, status effect, mark effect, public effect, and legal boundary are reviewable before a result arrives. |
| 4. Begin privately | Private Readiness creates no public certification status, no public failed-product listing, and no right to use the mark. |
| 5. Understand who can access the data | The applicable agreement and data notice identify the organizations, roles, purposes, retention, and legally required exceptions governing access. |
| 6. Have qualified existing evidence considered | Prior data are evaluated under published acceptance criteria rather than automatically discarded or automatically accepted. |
| 7. Receive a rule-based decision | Certification is decided against the published standard and governing edition — a mechanical ratio to a published limit — not a threshold negotiated after the result is known. |
| 8. Receive a scientifically defined confirmation route | Borderline, disputed, or analytically incomplete results follow the predefined measurement-uncertainty and confirmation rules; testing into compliance is prohibited. |
| 9. Appeal a determination | A published appeal process is available, including a tier referred to an independent reviewer body not controlled solely by the original decision-maker. |
| 10. Receive notice of standards changes | A versioned standard does not change silently. An ordinary change record identifies the redline, reason, publication and effective dates, transition period, and treatment of existing certificates; urgent corrections follow a separately identified route. |
| 11. Know that payment does not purchase a pass | Fees purchase evaluation and services. They create no right to certification and no compensation tied to a desired outcome. |
| 12. Keep private certification data out of the Heavy Metal Index | Applicant, supplier, formulation, laboratory, CAPA, and surveillance files do not enter the public Index merely because one institution operates both systems. |
| 13. Receive precise communication boundaries | Certified products receive approved and prohibited claim language, product scope, mark-use controls, limitations, and a verification record. |
| 14. Receive a defined improvement route | A product not yet certifiable receives a known route to confirmation, root-cause investigation, corrective action, sourcing or formulation change, and reassessment where applicable. |
| 15. Receive the decision record | The brand receives the standard edition, evidence relied upon, laboratory record, decision, status, certificate scope, and the material limitations needed to substantiate what it says. |
| 16. Exit without a fictional public failure | A brand may stop Private Readiness or withdraw an uncertified application without being placed in a public failed-applicant registry, subject to legal and contractual obligations. |
| 17. Know how nonessential data are handled | Before formal intake, the applicable notice and agreement identify retention, deletion, legal holds, archive, account closure, and the records that must remain for program integrity. Any public self-check the program offers is built to run in the reader's browser and transmit nothing. |
| 18. Be treated consistently | A product that meets the same published requirements receives the same decision regardless of brand size, commercial importance, or participation in another program. |

## Where these commitments come from

These are not aspirational. The load-bearing ones trace directly to ratified rules, which is why the program can hold to them:

- Rights 7, 8, 11, 18 — the **rule-based, unbuyable decision** — rest on the Locked Standards Method and Governing Principle 4: every limit is a mechanical ratio to a published government maximum, deterministic and reproducible, with no discretionary lever to sell. Testing into compliance is prohibited in Program Manual Parts 1, 2, and 4.
- Right 9 — the **independent appeal** — is Program Manual Part 2.9: a two-tier process whose second tier is referred to an independent three-reviewer body, and whose reversal binds the Program Operator.
- Right 10 — **change without ambush** — rests on Governing Principle 3 and the Standards Ratchet: standards ratchet tighter but never loosen, a single step is capped, changes run through a published transition period, and existing certifications are carried through it (the "Certified (Transitional)" status exists for exactly this).
- Right 14 — the **improvement route** — is the pre-certification remediation pathway. It is scoped deliberately: it does not, by itself, preserve a certificate that has not been issued, and post-certification consequences follow the governing edition. See [what happens if a product does not pass](/what-if-we-dont-pass).

Where a commitment reaches past what a ratified rule presently supports, the program's posture is to close the governance gap before asking a brand to rely on it — not to publish the promise and hope.

## Rights do not eliminate responsibilities

These commitments do not allow a brand to omit adverse data, alter samples, conceal changes, shop laboratories, retest without an approved basis, misuse the mark, or avoid a legal or contractual duty.

The brand remains responsible for accurate product and lot identification, complete disclosure of required information, valid chain of custody, formula and supplier change notification, legal compliance, corrective action, and removal of the mark when the governing status requires it.

## The practical test

Before a formal sample is submitted, a brand should be able to answer each of these without relying on a sales representative's verbal assurance:

| Question | Required source |
|---|---|
| Which standard applies? | Published standard and written scope confirmation. |
| What will this cost? | Itemized written quote, including the laboratory pass-through. |
| What happens to our records? | The applicable data notice and agreement, and the [trust and data section](/verify). |
| What happens at every result class? | Program Manual and the [result-consequence matrix](/what-if-we-dont-pass). |
| What can become public? | Visibility and verification rules. |
| What can we say if certified? | The [claims and mark-use guide](/mark). |
| What happens when the standard changes? | Governing Principle 3, the Standards Ratchet, and the applicable version record — see [program structure](/program/structure). |
| Who hears an appeal? | The published appeals procedure, Program Manual Part 2.9. |

When any answer is missing, the correct action is to close the governance gap before asking a brand to rely on it.
