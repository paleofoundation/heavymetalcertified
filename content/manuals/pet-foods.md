<section style="margin-bottom:44px"><h1 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:34px;line-height:1.15;color:#2b1f1f;margin:0 0 14px">Pet Foods — HMTc Program Manual</h1>
<p style="font-size:16px;line-height:1.7;color:#3f3a3a;max-width:80ch;margin:0 0 14px">The complete Heavy Metal Tested &amp; Certified standard for Category&nbsp;17, Pet Foods, edition 2026. Every limit on every subcategory is the strictest maximum level set by any credible government worldwide, converted to the product&rsquo;s native basis, with a disclosed read-across from the nearest government value where no government regulates the exact product. Nothing is invented; every number traces to a named sovereign limit in the <a href="/regulations" style="color:#1c43ad;text-decoration:none">regulation references</a>.</p>
<p style="font-size:13.5px;line-height:1.7;color:#736b6b;max-width:80ch;margin:0">Method: <a href="/program/structure" style="color:#1c43ad;text-decoration:none">government-limit default (Method v2.0)</a>. Pet food is legally feed, not food: its heavy-metal ceilings are set by feed law &mdash; EU Directive 2002/32/EC, FDA Center for Veterinary Medicine guidance, and the AAFCO model regulations &mdash; whose maximum levels run well above the corresponding human-food limits. HMTc does not certify to that feed floor. Each analyte is set to the strictest applicable human-food sovereign maximum for the nearest human-food analog, adopted as a voluntary human-grade over-compliance; the feed floor is disclosed on every row and never selects the number. Basis is never conflated &mdash; dry, as-sold products (kibble, semi-moist, freeze-dried, treats, chews) read across only from dry human analogs, and wet, as-consumed products (canned, raw, toppers, milk replacers, fish-based diets) only from as-consumed analogs, with no wet-to-dry conversion. Tier&nbsp;1 toxics (Pb, Cd, iAs, Hg) are zero-tolerance; Tier&nbsp;2 (tAs, Ni, Cr, Al, Sn) carry the program&rsquo;s 150% transitional allowance. Inorganic arsenic and hexavalent chromium operate as reflex speciation screens bounded by total arsenic and total chromium; methylmercury has no terrestrial biomagnification pathway and is bounded by total mercury; tin is material only for canned product, where it migrates from the tin-coated can, and is controlled by screen &mdash; not assigned an invented number &mdash; on every non-canned form.</p></section>

<section style="margin-bottom:44px"><h2 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:28px;color:#2b1f1f;border-bottom:1px solid #e8e1db;padding-bottom:10px;margin:0 0 18px">What is likely to struggle to meet this standard</h2><p style="font-size:13.5px;line-height:1.7;color:#736b6b;max-width:80ch;margin:0 0 16px">These subcategories carry a natural or process background that runs above the limit &mdash; the limit being the strictest government maximum for the product, never a percentile of market data (Method v2.0). A product is not disqualified by this: only its own tested result decides certification, and a well-sourced lot can sit well below the market. What the pooled occurrence literature shows is where the work is &mdash; which ingredients demand exceptional sourcing.</p><p style="font-size:13.5px;line-height:1.7;color:#3f3a3a;max-width:80ch;margin:0 0 14px"><span style="font-weight:500">Lead.</span> Lead is taken up from soil and can enter through adulteration; grains and rice, protein and collagen powders, spices, root and tuber vegetables, and some botanicals are common carriers. In this category: Complete &amp; balanced dry (kibble) (typically ~271&times; the 20 ppb limit).</p>
<p style="font-size:13.5px;line-height:1.7;color:#3f3a3a;max-width:80ch;margin:0 0 14px"><span style="font-weight:500">Mercury.</span> Large and predatory fish and other marine ingredients accumulate methylmercury up the food chain. In this category: Complete &amp; balanced dry (kibble) (typically ~65&times; the 20 ppb limit).</p>
<p style="font-size:13.5px;line-height:1.7;color:#3f3a3a;max-width:80ch;margin:0 0 14px"><span style="font-weight:500">Cadmium.</span> Cocoa, leafy greens, sunflower and other seeds, organ meats, shellfish, seaweed, and some root vegetables concentrate cadmium from soil and seawater. In this category: Complete &amp; balanced dry (kibble) (typically ~1.6&times; the 40 ppb limit).</p>
<p style="font-size:12px;line-height:1.6;color:#a9a1a1;max-width:80ch;margin:14px 0 0">Multipliers are the ratio of the pooled occurrence median to the published limit; occurrence characterises the market only and carries no compliance weight. See <a href="/hard-to-meet" style="color:#1c43ad;text-decoration:none">the cross-category summary</a>.</p></section>
