<section style="margin-bottom:44px"><h1 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:34px;line-height:1.15;color:#2b1f1f;margin:0 0 14px">Grains, Cereals, and Rice Products — HMTc Program Manual</h1>
<p style="font-size:16px;line-height:1.7;color:#3f3a3a;max-width:80ch;margin:0 0 14px">The complete Heavy Metal Tested &amp; Certified standard for Category&nbsp;3, Grains, Cereals, and Rice Products, edition 2026. Every limit on every subcategory is the strictest maximum level set by any credible government worldwide, converted to the product&rsquo;s native basis, with a disclosed read-across from the nearest government value where no government regulates the exact product. Nothing is invented; every number traces to a named sovereign limit in the <a href="/regulations" style="color:#1c43ad;text-decoration:none">regulation references</a>.</p>
<p style="font-size:13.5px;line-height:1.7;color:#736b6b;max-width:80ch;margin:0">Method: <a href="/program/structure" style="color:#1c43ad;text-decoration:none">government-limit default (Method v2.0)</a>. Grain, cereal, and rice limits are taken at face value in each product&rsquo;s native basis (as sold for solids; as consumed for rice beverages); there is no reconstitution conversion. The rice / non-rice split is the clean / dirty structure, and it falls straight out of the government limits themselves: every rice subcategory carries a binding inorganic-arsenic maximum level set by product form (EU 2023/915), while non-rice cereals resolve inorganic arsenic as not-material because arsenic does not hyperaccumulate in them. Total arsenic (tAs) carries the government cereal / rice total-arsenic ceiling as an independent limit; the inorganic fraction is the toxic species and binds first on rice. Tier&nbsp;1 toxics (Pb, Cd, iAs, Hg) are zero-tolerance; Tier&nbsp;2 (tAs, Cr-VI, Ni, Sn, Al) carry the program&rsquo;s 150% transitional allowance. Methylmercury is bounded by the total-mercury ceiling; hexavalent chromium is controlled by reflex speciation on total chromium; tin is material only for canned product (routed to the canned-goods category); aluminium has no government grain maximum. The rice inorganic-arsenic maximum for products destined for infants and young children (100 ppb) is stricter and is set in the Infant and Child Foods volume, not here.</p></section>
