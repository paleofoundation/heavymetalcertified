## Start here if you make a product

Heavy Metal Tested &amp; Certified certifies a product against a published number, on a ten-analyte panel, in the form it is actually sold in. For a brand, the mark is a claim a buyer or a retailer can check: not a reassurance. This page routes you to what you need to decide whether to certify, in the order a brand usually reads it.

## What the mark does for a brand

- [What the mark says](/mark): the exact claim the seal makes on a package, and the scope line it must appear with, so the claim can be checked rather than trusted.
- [The Premium Promise](/briefings/brief-6-premium-promise): why a checkable heavy-metal claim can support a price where a process claim cannot.
- [The cost of being uncertified](/briefings/brief-4-cost-of-being-uncertified): the exposure a four-metal mark or an untested SKU carries as governments write new limits.
- [What organic doesn&rsquo;t say](/briefings/brief-3-what-organic-doesnt-say): why an organic certificate is not a heavy-metal result.

## Before you apply

- [Is my product eligible?](/standards): the subcategory standards register. A product is eligible the moment a standard exists for its subcategory; a brand does not wait for the rest of its category.
- [How a limit is set, and how testing works](/program/structure): the derivation, the ten-analyte panel, and the five certification statuses.
- [How HMTc compares](/compare): coverage against NSF, USP, Clean Label Project and the rest, so you know exactly what the mark adds.

## Apply

- [Apply for certification](/apply): the application, what a lab report has to show, and the fee structure. Fees are annual and scale with revenue, so a small brand is not priced at an enterprise rate; two- and three-year terms take up to 20% off.
- [Fee calculator](https://paleofoundation.com/pricing): an estimate before you commit.
