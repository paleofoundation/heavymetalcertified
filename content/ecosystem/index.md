## The proof problem

Heavy metals in food aren’t a content problem. They’re a proof problem.

Having a test report is not the same as having a settled purchasing decision. Having a qualified ingredient lot is not the same as having a certified finished product. Having certification is not the same as having authorization to use a mark.

HMI, TECRID, VLE, and HMTc connect public knowledge, authenticated laboratory evidence, bounded-lot qualification, and finished-product certification. Each unit has a defined job. Together, they establish a clearer relationship between the requirement, the evidence, the purchasing decision, and the public claim.

## Four connected units. Four distinct responsibilities.

### HMI: Heavy Metal Index

Understand the basis for the requirement.

Heavy Metal Index provides public knowledge and standards gravity through DOI pathways, government floors, and occurrence evidence.

Its role is to make the underlying knowledge and standards context accessible. What requirements apply? What occurrence evidence informs the concern? What is the basis for asking an ingredient or finished product to meet a particular standard?

That context matters, but it is not a pass decision for a particular lot. Public evidence about a food category cannot establish that a specific quantity meets a specific purchasing requirement.

[Explore Heavy Metal Index](https://heavymetalindex.com) · [Read the HMI paper](/ecosystem/hmi)

### TECRID

Check the laboratory evidence.

TECRID is the authenticated lab-evidence spine. A result can be checked to its issuing laboratory, structured measurands, version history, and current status.

That means the evidence question can extend beyond the contents of an attachment: who issued this result, what was measured, which version is being reviewed, and what is its current status?

TECRID does not certify products, prove representative sampling, or replace ISO 17025. Authenticating a result does not establish that the tested sample represents an entire lot or that a finished product qualifies for a certification claim.

The core trust layer is intentionally not a tax on credibility.

[Explore TECRID](https://tecrid.com) · [Read the TECRID paper](/ecosystem/tecrid)

### VLE: Verified Lot Exchange

Qualify a defined quantity against a defined purchasing requirement.

VLE connects a bounded ingredient or product lot to a frozen Compliance Profile, sampling, and TECRID-backed evidence before reservation or trade.

The sequence is explicit:

Nominate a bounded lot → Freeze a Compliance Profile → Sample → Establish TECRID-backed evidence → QUALIFIED only for that bounded quantity and profile → Reserve or trade

QUALIFIED is not a general endorsement of a supplier, a product name, or every future delivery. The decision applies only to the bounded quantity and the frozen profile it was evaluated against.

VLE is not finished-product certification. It is also not metals-only. The required testing panel follows what the purchase requires.

[Explore Verified Lot Exchange](https://vle.exchange) · [Read the VLE paper](/ecosystem/vle)

### HMTc: Heavy Metal Tested & Certified

Support a finished-product certification claim against published numeric standards.

HMTc addresses the finished product and its certification claim surfaces. Its role is certification against published numeric standards, not merely confirmation that a laboratory report exists.

An authenticated result is not HMTc certification. A QUALIFIED ingredient lot is not HMTc certification. Neither should be presented as a substitute for the finished-product determination.

Certification and mark authorization are separate. A certification decision must not be treated as automatic permission to display the mark.

## How the units fit together

HMI supplies public knowledge and standards context. TECRID makes laboratory evidence checkable. VLE connects that evidence to a bounded quantity and frozen purchasing profile. HMTc separately addresses finished-product certification against published numeric standards.

These responsibilities are connected, but they are not interchangeable.

A purchasing requirement needs an identified basis. A result needs an authenticated issuer and a checkable status. A lot decision needs a defined quantity, a frozen profile, and appropriate sampling. A finished-product claim needs the certification basis appropriate to that claim.

The point is not to make one record answer every question. It is to make clear which evidence supports which decision.

## What this changes for each role

### Brands

Resolve ingredient uncertainty before it becomes a finished-product claims problem.

VLE provides a defined qualification process for the quantity being purchased. TECRID makes the supporting laboratory evidence checkable. HMTc separately addresses finished-product certification.

This gives brands a clearer distinction between evidence used to buy an ingredient and evidence supporting a finished-product claim. One should not silently become the other.

### Suppliers

Offer a specific qualified quantity, not an unsupported general assurance.

A supplier can distinguish “we have a report” from “this bounded quantity is QUALIFIED against this frozen Compliance Profile.”

TECRID supports the laboratory-evidence question. VLE supports the bounded-lot purchasing decision. The scope stays attached to the quantity and profile, rather than extending to everything the supplier sells.

### Retailers and buyers

Review a defined decision rather than reconstruct one from attachments.

The relevant questions become explicit: which lot, which quantity, which requirements, which evidence version, and which current status?

VLE addresses lot qualification. TECRID addresses evidence authentication. HMTc addresses finished-product certification where that is the claim under review. None of these distinctions requires buyers to abandon their own necessary testing or quality requirements.

### Laboratories

Keep the issued result distinct from claims made about a product.

TECRID allows a result to be checked to the issuing laboratory, structured measurands, version history, and current status.

That gives the laboratory’s evidence a defined role without turning it into a claim that the laboratory certified a product, established representative sampling, or replaced the responsibilities addressed by ISO 17025.

### Insurers and brokers

Examine a more clearly defined factual basis.

The network distinguishes authenticated laboratory evidence, bounded-lot qualification, finished-product certification, and mark authorization.

Those distinctions can support a more specific review of what was tested, what quantity was qualified, and what claim is being made. They do not determine insurance coverage, policy terms, or premiums.

### Defense counsel

Separate the evidence record from the commercial claim.

Issuer authentication, version history, current status, bounded qualification, and published certification standards provide distinct facts for review.

Counsel can examine what a record supports without treating a test result as product certification or a certification decision as mark authorization. These are factual inputs, not promises about legal outcomes.

### End consumers

Understand what the finished-product claim actually means.

HMTc directs attention to certification against published numeric standards rather than an undefined statement that a product was “tested.”

The relevant distinction is the scope of the finished-product certification. It is not a general claim that metals are absent or that every possible product question has been answered.

## Move existing failure spend upstream

The commercial goal is straightforward: redirect spending already associated with metals failures, rejected lots, repeated testing, and PDF/COA theater toward QUALIFIED lots and checkable proof.

That does not mean every second test is unnecessary. It means the essential questions should be resolved earlier: what is being purchased, what it must meet, how it will be sampled, and which evidence supports the decision.

The aim is not less rigor. It is earlier rigor, applied to a defined quantity and requirement before reservation or trade.

## The order is deliberate

Freeze the decision basis. Establish checkable evidence. Keep numeric claims within their supported scope. Address mark authorization and marketing afterward.
