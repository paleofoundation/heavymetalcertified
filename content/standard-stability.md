A brand can improve only when the rule is stable enough to plan against and honest enough to improve over time. Heavy Metal Tested & Certified therefore treats version identity, notice, transition, and decision history as part of the standard rather than administrative detail.

## The current rule

| Question | Rule |
|---|---|
| Which edition governs a certification decision? | The dated standard and manual edition named in the decision record and certificate. |
| Can a later edition silently change the result of an earlier tested lot? | No. The original decision remains tied to its named edition. |
| Can a limit be loosened because a brand finds it difficult? | No. Substantive limit changes use the ratchet and move toward tighter control as evidence and analytical feasibility support it. |
| What happens to an existing certificate when a standard tightens? | Existing certifications are carried through the published transition; the change notice states the effective date, required evidence, and status treatment. |
| Can the program correct an error? | Yes. A correction must be identified, versioned, explained, and separated from an ordinary evidence-driven tightening. |

## What every substantive change notice must contain

Before a new edition governs new work, the public record should state:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Prior and new editions | Makes the exact rule change identifiable. |
| Redline or cell-level change list | Shows what moved without requiring a brand to reconstruct two documents. |
| Reason and trigger | Distinguishes new evidence, regulatory change, analytical feasibility, surveillance signal, error correction, and governance change. |
| Affected products, forms, analytes, and markets | Prevents a broad category label from hiding a narrow change. |
| Publication, effective, and review dates | Gives the change a visible clock. |
| Existing-certificate treatment | States the transition status, mark implications, and renewal consequence. |
| New testing and evidence treatment | States whether existing results remain usable and what additional work is required. |
| Cost treatment | Identifies who bears transition-specific review, testing, artwork, or verification costs. |
| Appeal and correction route | Gives the brand a controlled way to challenge a factual or procedural error. |

## Ordinary tightening and urgent correction

An ordinary evidence-driven change should be forecast and published before it controls a new decision. The notice period and transition length are set in the change record because the operational impact differs by analyte, method, product form, packaging cycle, and surveillance schedule.

An urgent legal, analytical, or integrity issue may require a faster correction. The program should identify the urgency, evidence, interim control, affected certificates, and review date rather than using an emergency label to avoid explanation.

## What stability does not mean

Stability is not a promise that a limit will never change, that a legal limit cannot take immediate effect, or that a certificate continues regardless of later surveillance data. It means the program does not move the rule in secret, apply a later edition to an earlier decision without a transition record, or conceal the consequence of change.

Status changes caused by a product’s own later test result follow the published surveillance and consequence rules. They are different from a program-wide standards revision.

## The decision record a brand should keep

For each certified product, the substantiation file should preserve the product and form, markets, tested lots, laboratory and method, accepted evidence, governing standard and manual editions, decision date, status, certificate, authorized scope line, mark artwork approval, transition notices, surveillance results, CAPA, appeal, and renewal history.

That record makes a claim explainable even after the public standard has advanced.
