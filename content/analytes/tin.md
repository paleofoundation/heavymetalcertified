<section style="margin-bottom:40px"><h1 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:38px;line-height:1.1;color:#2b1f1f;margin:0 0 8px">Tin</h1><div style="font-family:'IBM Plex Mono',monospace;font-size:12px;letter-spacing:.04em;color:#b7aeae;margin:0 0 18px">50 &middot; Sn &middot; Total elemental tin</div><p style="font-size:17px;line-height:1.7;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0;max-width:80ch">Inorganic tin migrating from tinplate cans produces only an acute gastrointestinal effect, with a wide margin between regulated canned-food concentrations and the dose that reliably irritates the gut in controlled human challenge, so its maximum level functions chiefly as a sentinel for can-lining integrity rather than a guard against chronic or cumulative toxicity.</p></section>

<section id="toxicology" style="margin-bottom:52px"><h2 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:30px;line-height:1.2;color:#2b1f1f;border-bottom:1px solid #e8e1db;padding-bottom:10px;margin:0 0 20px">Toxicological basis</h2><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">The critical endpoint for ingested inorganic tin (the form that dissolves from tinplate into acidic canned foods) is local acute gastrointestinal irritation — nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps — acting on the gut mucosa; inorganic tin is poorly absorbed (roughly 5 percent, with more than 90 percent excreted in feces) and does not accumulate systemically. Controlled human single-dose challenges placed the robust symptomatic threshold at approximately 1,000 to 1,400 ppm tin in canned fruit juices, well above the canned-product caps set in any major jurisdiction; that robust threshold is the basis for the "wide margin" framing. Regulators nonetheless set maximum levels lower, near the range at which the most sensitive individuals have reported gastrointestinal effects — the JECFA evaluation records irritation in some individuals at concentrations near 150 mg/kg in beverages and 250 mg/kg in other canned foods — so the maximum levels sit at that sensitive-individual reported-effect floor rather than at the robust controlled threshold. Organotin compounds are a structurally separate and far more toxic class (lipophilic, well-absorbed, with immune, central-nervous-system and reproductive endpoints at exposures about three orders of magnitude lower), but they arise mainly from seafood and PVC contact materials rather than from the canned-food pathway that the certification's total-elemental-tin measure targets. The principal vulnerable group is infants and young children fed canned baby food, whom EU 2023/915 protects with a 50 mg/kg cap set at one-quarter of the general canned-food cap.</p></section>
<section id="regulation" style="margin-bottom:52px"><h2 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:30px;line-height:1.2;color:#2b1f1f;border-bottom:1px solid #e8e1db;padding-bottom:10px;margin:0 0 20px">How it is regulated</h2><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Binding maximum levels are concentration caps on canned product. EU 2023/915 sets the strictest published caps (200 mg/kg canned food other than beverages, 100 mg/kg canned beverages, 50 mg/kg canned infant/baby foods and formulas) and generally governs; Codex CXS 193-1995 is looser (250 and 150 mg/kg, JECFA-derived). The US FDA sets no action level for tin in food, addressing it instead through Total Diet Study surveillance and food-contact-material guidance — so where the strictest sovereign limit must be found for a non-canned or infant product, the standard reads across from the nearest government value rather than inventing one. In the HMTc infant-and-child-food standards the binding value is the strictest government ML converted to the row's native basis (for example, India FSSAI binds the teething-and-snacks Sn row at 5,000 µg/kg as-sold). See the cert /regulations/ derivation pages — eu-2023-915, codex-cxs-193, india-fssai, and us-fda-c2z — for the side-by-side sovereign comparison; the standard pages, not this page, carry the full number set.</p></section>
<section id="hmtc" style="margin-bottom:52px"><h2 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:30px;line-height:1.2;color:#2b1f1f;border-bottom:1px solid #e8e1db;padding-bottom:10px;margin:0 0 20px">How the HMTc program treats it</h2><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Tin is a Tier 2 analyte, certified as "total elemental tin" under the program's 150% transitional allowance within the Status A–E framework, rather than as a zero-tolerance Tier 1 toxic — a treatment consistent with its acute-only, high-margin, non-cumulative gastrointestinal endpoint. No speciation trigger applies to tin in the certification program: unlike arsenic (reflex to iAs), mercury (MeHg), or chromium (reflex hexavalent-chromium speciation on total-Cr above 100 ppb), tin is measured and limited as total elemental tin. Its operational role is a sentinel for packaging and can-lining integrity — an elevated total-tin result flags a corroding, under-lacquered, or long-stored can rather than a systemic health hazard. Grounded in standards/teething-and-snacks.md (Sn listed Tier 2, HMTc limit 5,000 ppb bound by India FSSAI; the Tier note reads "Tier 2 (Ni, Al, Sn, Cr) carry a 150% transitional allowance under the program's Status A–E framework") and the infant-and-child-foods.md manual method note ("Tier 2 (Ni, Al, Sn, Cr) carry the program's 150% transitional allowance").</p></section>
