<section style="margin-bottom:40px"><h1 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:38px;line-height:1.1;color:#2b1f1f;margin:0 0 8px">Aluminum</h1><div style="font-family:'IBM Plex Mono',monospace;font-size:12px;letter-spacing:.04em;color:#b7aeae;margin:0 0 18px">13 &middot; Al &middot; Total elemental aluminum</div><p style="font-size:17px;line-height:1.7;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0;max-width:80ch">Aluminium is limited because chronic dietary intake accumulates against a low tolerable weekly intake derived from animal neurotoxic, reproductive, and developmental endpoints — corroborated in humans by dialysis encephalopathy and osteomalacia — with renally impaired people, frequent antacid users, and formula-fed infants the most exposed; the resulting body burden is controllable through water treatment and process controls.</p></section>

<section id="toxicology" style="margin-bottom:52px"><h2 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:30px;line-height:1.2;color:#2b1f1f;border-bottom:1px solid #e8e1db;padding-bottom:10px;margin:0 0 20px">Toxicological basis</h2><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Aluminium is a non-essential metal whose principal toxicological targets are the nervous system (neurotoxicity) and bone. Because gastrointestinal absorption is low (typically less than 1 percent) and excretion is renal, the clearest human disease — dialysis encephalopathy (progressive dementia, dysarthria, myoclonus, seizures) and dialysis osteomalacia — arose in hemodialysis patients whose impaired renal excretion and parenteral dialysate route bypassed the GI barrier, making these the proof-of-concept anchor for aluminium's neuro- and bone toxicity (ATSDR 2008). For the general oral-exposure population the regulatory tolerable weekly intake rests on animal neurotoxic, reproductive, and developmental endpoints rather than robust human dietary-cohort data; EFSA judged that this TWI is likely exceeded in a significant part of the European population. The most vulnerable are individuals with impaired renal function (reduced excretion drives body-burden accumulation), frequent users of aluminium-containing antacids (100 to 1000 mg Al/day), and infants on soy-based formula (higher aluminium than milk-based formula or breast milk).</p></section>
<section id="regulation" style="margin-bottom:52px"><h2 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:30px;line-height:1.2;color:#2b1f1f;border-bottom:1px solid #e8e1db;padding-bottom:10px;margin:0 0 20px">How it is regulated</h2><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Aluminium is unusual among the HMTc analytes in that almost no government sets a per-food concentration maximum level: the derivation tables on the cert standard pages (see <a href="/standards/teething-and-snacks" style="color:#1c43ad">/standards/teething-and-snacks</a> and <a href="/standards/infant-formula-powder" style="color:#1c43ad">/standards/infant-formula-powder</a>) show blank Al cells for the EU (<a href="/regulations/eu-2023-915" style="color:#1c43ad">/regulations/eu-2023-915</a>), UK (<a href="/regulations/uk-1881-2006" style="color:#1c43ad">/regulations/uk-1881-2006</a>), Codex (<a href="/regulations/codex-cxs-193" style="color:#1c43ad">/regulations/codex-cxs-193</a>), Canada, China (<a href="/regulations/china-gb-2762" style="color:#1c43ad">/regulations/china-gb-2762</a>), India (<a href="/regulations/india-fssai" style="color:#1c43ad">/regulations/india-fssai</a>), Brazil, and the US FDA (<a href="/regulations/us-fda-c2z" style="color:#1c43ad">/regulations/us-fda-c2z</a>). The only government aluminium food ML in any jurisdiction is Australia/New Zealand FSANZ Food Standards Code Schedule 19 (<a href="/regulations/au-nz-fsanz" style="color:#1c43ad">/regulations/au-nz-fsanz</a>): 500 µg/kg for infant formula, follow-on formula, and special-medical-purpose products for infants other than preterm products, and 1000 µg/kg for soy-based infant formula, both on a portion-ordinarily-consumed basis. Everywhere else, control is exercised through intake-based reference values rather than a food ML — EFSA's TWI (1 mg Al/kg b.w./week, 2008), JECFA's current PTWI (2 mg Al/kg b.w./week, 74th JECFA meeting 2011), and ATSDR's oral MRL (1 mg Al/kg/day) — which govern total weekly intake, not the concentration in a single food. EFSA and JECFA are separate bodies and their reference values genuinely differ (EFSA 1, JECFA 2). Because no government regulates most product-analyte cells for aluminium, HMTc governs by read-across from the FSANZ value; see the /regulations/ pages for the maximum-level derivations and the Index page (/metals/aluminum) for the intake-based reference values.</p></section>
<section id="hmtc" style="margin-bottom:52px"><h2 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:30px;line-height:1.2;color:#2b1f1f;border-bottom:1px solid #e8e1db;padding-bottom:10px;margin:0 0 20px">How the HMTc program treats it</h2><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Aluminium (certification species: total elemental aluminium; no speciation trigger, unlike As→iAs, Hg→MeHg, or Cr→Cr-VI) is a Tier 2 analyte in the HMTc program, grouped with Ni, Sn, and Cr. Tier 2 analytes carry a 150% transitional allowance under the program's Status A-E framework, distinguishing them from the Tier 1 zero-tolerance toxics (Pb, Cd, iAs, Hg) that have no established safe threshold. Because only FSANZ Schedule 19 sets any government aluminium ML, HMTc limits are set by disclosed read-across from that value: the teething-and-snacks standard applies 500 µg/kg at face value on an as-sold basis (the 8:1 reconstitution factor is formula-specific and is not applied to a non-formula product), while the infant-formula-powder standard converts the FSANZ portion-consumed values at the 8:1 powder-to-liquid ratio to 4000 µg/kg (non-soy) and 8000 µg/kg (soy-based). The Status A-E tiering, the 150% Tier-2 transitional allowance, the regulatory hard-stop, basis conversion, and lot testing operate downstream of the limit regardless of how the number is set.</p></section>
