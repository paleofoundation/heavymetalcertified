<section style="margin-bottom:40px"><h1 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:38px;line-height:1.1;color:#2b1f1f;margin:0 0 8px">Nickel</h1><div style="font-family:'IBM Plex Mono',monospace;font-size:12px;letter-spacing:.04em;color:#b7aeae;margin:0 0 18px">28 &middot; Ni &middot; Total elemental nickel</div><p style="font-size:17px;line-height:1.7;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0;max-width:80ch">Nickel is limited because EFSA's 2020 reassessment set a chronic tolerable daily intake of 13 µg Ni/kg body weight/day on a reproductive-developmental endpoint that mean European dietary intake already exceeds, and because sensitized individuals react to oral doses far below that; contamination is largely controllable through sourcing and process control.</p></section>

<section id="toxicology" style="margin-bottom:52px"><h2 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:30px;line-height:1.2;color:#2b1f1f;border-bottom:1px solid #e8e1db;padding-bottom:10px;margin:0 0 20px">Toxicological basis</h2><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">The chronic dietary concern is reproductive and developmental: EFSA's TDI is anchored on increased post-implantation loss (with reduced fetal weight and viability) in rats, making women planning or during pregnancy, and children and toddlers with higher per-kilogram intake, the sensitive windows. A separate, more acute concern is systemic contact dermatitis (eczematous flare-up) in nickel-sensitized individuals, who react to oral nickel well below the chronic TDI. Nickel compounds are also respiratory carcinogens (lung and nasal cancer) but this is an occupational-inhalation endpoint documented in refinery, plating and stainless-steel cohorts; typical dietary nickel intake has not been associated with cancer outcomes. See the Heavy Metal Index nickel page for the full literature synthesis.</p></section>
<section id="regulation" style="margin-bottom:52px"><h2 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:30px;line-height:1.2;color:#2b1f1f;border-bottom:1px solid #e8e1db;padding-bottom:10px;margin:0 0 20px">How it is regulated</h2><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">The binding maximum level in force for infant and child foods is the EU's, adopted under Method v2.0 (government-limit default) as the strictest sovereign limit by lookup. The EU is effectively the only jurisdiction that regulates nickel in food (limits introduced by Reg (EU) 2024/1987 amending 2023/915, Annex 3.6): liquid infant formula 100, non-soy formula powder 250, soy-isolate formula powder 400, baby food except juices 500, cereals 800, and processed cereal-based baby food / pseudocereals and millet 3000 µg/kg (see cert <a href="/regulations/eu-2023-915" style="color:#1c43ad">/regulations/eu-2023-915</a>). No US/FDA, Codex, UK, Canada, China, India, Brazil or AU/NZ nickel food ML exists, so the EU value governs across the program and, where no government regulates the exact product, a disclosed read-across from the nearest EU value is applied rather than an invented number. For teething and finger snacks the EU processed-cereal-based value (3000 µg/kg) binds; the law's own formulation split (250 cow-milk vs 400 soy powder) is adopted verbatim per Method v2.0 rather than derived. Do not restate the full derivation table — see the cert standards pages and /regulations/ for the side-by-side.</p></section>
<section id="hmtc" style="margin-bottom:52px"><h2 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:30px;line-height:1.2;color:#2b1f1f;border-bottom:1px solid #e8e1db;padding-bottom:10px;margin:0 0 20px">How the HMTc program treats it</h2><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Nickel is a Tier 2 analyte in the HMTc program (with Al, Sn, Cr), not a Tier 1 zero-tolerance toxic. Under Method v2.0 the government limit is adopted directly for Tier 2 metals (occurrence pooling is reserved for Tier 1 toxics and no-ML cells), and Tier 2 analytes carry the program's 150% transitional allowance under the Status A–E framework (Status B = public transitional certification), giving brands a defined runway to reach the government floor. The certification species is Total elemental nickel; nickel carries NO speciation trigger — unlike arsenic (iAs), mercury (MeHg/tHg) and chromium (reflex Cr-VI speciation above 100 ppb). The regulatory hard-stop still applies: any actual legal-limit violation blocks certification regardless of HMTc status. The program frames nickel as largely controllable through ingredient sourcing and process control.</p></section>
