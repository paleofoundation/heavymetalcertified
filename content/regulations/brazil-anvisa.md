<section id="quick" style="margin-bottom:52px"><h2 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:30px;line-height:1.2;color:#2b1f1f;border-bottom:1px solid #e8e1db;padding-bottom:10px;margin:0 0 20px">Quick read</h2><p style="font-size:16.5px;line-height:1.78;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 18px;max-width:72ch">Resolução da Diretoria Colegiada (RDC) nº 722, de 1º de julho de 2022 (general principles, scope and analytical methods for maximum tolerated limits — LMT — of contaminants in food; revokes RDC nº 487/2021, which had replaced RDC nº 42/2013); numeric LMT tables set by the companion Instrução Normativa (IN) nº 160, de 1º de julho de 2022 (which itself consolidated and revoked IN nº 88/2021, IN nº 115/2021 and IN nº 152/2022), as amended by IN nº 351, de 18 de março de 2025 is a binding contaminant standard in force in Brazil. It sets 25 infant and child food maximum levels across 4 of the eight HMTc metals — lead, inorganic arsenic, cadmium, and tin — transcribed here from RDC nº 722/2022 (DOU nº 126, 6 jul. 2022, Seção 1); IN nº 160/2022 (Anexo I — metais); IN nº 351/2025 (altera Anexo I da IN 160/2022); base Mercosul: MERCOSUL/GMC/RES. Nº 12/11 as amended by Res. GMC Nº 18/21. HMTc records it because a certification standard adopts the strictest maximum level any credible government sets worldwide, and cites this instrument wherever it holds that value. This page records the law; it does not set or justify any HMTc threshold.</p></section>
