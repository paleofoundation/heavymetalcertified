## How limits are set

The method begins with the product form. Each subcategory is assessed in its own native basis, powder as placed on market, ready-to-feed as consumed, dry goods as sold, so that no two products are compared on an unstated basis. The default limit for any analyte is the strictest maximum level set by a credible government regulator, converted into that basis. This is a lookup, not a novel computation, which is what makes it defensible.

Occurrence evidence enters selectively. For the four zero-tolerance toxics, and for the cells no government regulates, the program sets its own value from the pooled occurrence record and floors it against the government maximum so the published number is the stricter of the two. A value publishes only after it clears the program's evidence, conversion, coverage, method, and governance checks. The full derivation, the calibration behind the occurrence percentiles, and the publication gates are documented in [how the program works](/program/structure#calibration), and the two routes available when a product does not yet meet a value are set out in [the two remediation pathways](/program/two-pathways).

## What the program measures

The Big 8 are lead, cadmium, arsenic, mercury, nickel, tin, aluminum, and chromium. Arsenic and mercury are resolved by species where the standard requires it, inorganic arsenic and methylmercury, which produces a ten-analyte reporting panel. Every certified product is measured against every applicable analyte in its standard; there is no four-metal short screen.

Open the [complete analyte panel](/analytes) for the reason each metal is controlled, or read the governing toxicity directly for [lead](/analytes/lead), [cadmium](/analytes/cadmium), [arsenic](/analytes/arsenic), and [mercury](/analytes/mercury).

## Evidence and policy

Heavy Metal Certified publishes the standards and the certification record. The independent [Heavy Metal Index](https://heavymetalindex.com) holds the peer-reviewed literature layer that supports the occurrence and toxicology record, kept as a separate property by design so the standard rests on evidence it does not itself author. Government instruments stay identifiable as law or guidance and are never presented as program thresholds.

Three surfaces carry the public evidence: the [regulatory register](/regulations) of primary-source maximum levels and the bodies that issued them, the [research library](/articles) of certification-focused analyses, and the [standards briefings](/briefings) written for legal, retail, finance, and standards audiences. For what certification is and what it asserts, start with [what certification is](/heavy-metal-certification).
