Discovery is useful only when a brand knows where the record goes, who can see it, what can become public, and which obligations cannot be contracted away. This page separates the controls that exist on the current public website from the controls that must govern a formal applicant file.

## The current public website

The readiness check on this website runs in the visitor’s browser. Answers are not submitted to Heavy Metal Tested & Certified, do not create an applicant account, and are not retained by the tool. Ordinary hosting and security logs may still record standard request metadata when a page is visited; the form answers are not included in those requests.

The current administrative submission system is separate. A brand should review the notice and agreement presented by that system before providing contact information, formulas, laboratory reports, or other confidential records.

## The formal data lifecycle

Before a future on-domain intake accepts private files, the applicable agreement and data notice must identify each stage below.

| Stage | Data involved | Required control |
|---|---|---|
| Scope and readiness | Product form, intended consumer, markets, portfolio, existing-evidence inventory | Minimum necessary collection; nonpublic participant status; purpose stated before collection. |
| Application | Legal entity, contacts, facilities, products, formulas, suppliers, markets, agreements | Role-based access, audit history, and a record of the governing terms. |
| Testing | Sample identity, custody, laboratory reports, methods, limits of detection and quantitation, uncertainty, lots | Access limited to the decision and quality functions that need the record. |
| Decision | Governing standard, accepted evidence, gaps, findings, status, appeal, CAPA | Versioned decision record; no outcome changed by fee tier or commercial preference. |
| Certification | Product, form, certificate, standard edition, dates, authorized claims and mark rights | Only the verification fields authorized by the program’s public-record rule become public. |
| Surveillance and renewal | Later lots, changes, alerts, CAPA, trend and renewal record | Status and mark consequences follow the published rules, not a private promise to preserve a certificate. |
| Closure | Account, certificate, expired records, legal hold, audit evidence | Retention and deletion follow the agreement, published schedule, law, and program-integrity needs. |

## Who may receive a private record

Access is purpose-limited. The agreement and data notice must identify the operator, certifier, qualified laboratories, technology providers, professional advisers, and other processors that can receive a record, together with the purpose of access.

A private readiness or certification file is not a public marketing asset. It is also not automatically protected by attorney-client privilege or work-product doctrine. A brand that needs a counsel-directed privileged investigation should structure that work with its own counsel before submitting records.

## What can become public

The program does not publish a list of Private Readiness participants or unsuccessful applicants. A readiness gap is not a public certification failure.

When a product is certified, the public verification record may identify the product, form, certificate number, governing standard and edition, certification status, effective dates, authorized mark scope, and any disclosure the governing rules require. It does not publish the brand’s complete formula, supplier file, laboratory report, or confidential measured concentrations merely because a certificate exists.

Directory browsing and certificate verification are different. A brand may control optional directory visibility where the governing manual permits it; a certificate or product code deliberately presented to a customer must still resolve through the authoritative verification route.

## Law, regulators, recalls, and compulsory process

Commercial confidentiality has limits. It does not delay a recall, override a reporting duty, defeat a legal hold, nullify a subpoena or court order, prevent a regulator request, or relieve a brand of retailer and contractual notice duties.

The program may preserve or produce records when legally required and should notify and cooperate with the brand where the law and circumstances permit. The brand remains responsible for its own legal advice and market obligations.

## Retention, export, correction, and deletion

The current public website does not announce a numerical retention period that has not been ratified. Before formal on-domain intake opens, the governing notice must state:

| Right or control | What must be clear |
|---|---|
| Access and export | Which applicant and certificate records the brand can retrieve, in what format, and on what schedule. |
| Correction | How factual errors are challenged without altering the original audit history. |
| Deletion | Which nonessential records can be deleted after withdrawal or closure and how deletion is confirmed. |
| Required retention | Which decision, certificate, mark-control, appeal, audit, legal, or regulatory records must remain and why. |
| Legal hold | How ordinary deletion stops when preservation is required. |
| Provider deletion | How downstream processors are instructed when deletion is permitted. |

Until those terms are presented and accepted, a brand should not infer a retention promise from general privacy language.

## The Heavy Metal Index firewall

Applicant files, brand laboratory reports, formulas, supplier records, remediation documents, and certification decisions do not become public Heavy Metal Index evidence. HMI is a separately governed literature index built from public and independently admissible sources.

Information can cross that boundary only through a separately defined, consented, de-identified, and governed process that prevents participant identification and preserves the Index’s evidence rules. No such transfer is implied by applying, testing, remediating, or certifying.
