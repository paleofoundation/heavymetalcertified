## Start here for due diligence

Regulatory and quality teams check whether a certification&rsquo;s scientific basis is traceable to the literature; legal teams price it as litigation insurance and ask whether it would survive a plaintiff&rsquo;s expert. HMTc is built for that reading: every published limit traces to a named government maximum or a documented occurrence pool, in the product&rsquo;s native basis, and the derivation is published rather than self-asserted. This page routes you to the record.

## How the limits are set — and traced

- [How every limit is derived](/program/structure) &mdash; the derivation, the five publication gates, and the rationale tag every published value carries.
- [Regulation references](/regulations) &mdash; the primary-source sovereign instruments a standard adopts when a jurisdiction binds a limit. A record of law, not an HMTc threshold.
- [The analyte panel and toxicology basis](/analytes) &mdash; for each metal, the government and expert-body record behind its inclusion.
- [The standards register](/standards) &mdash; the numeric limits themselves, per subcategory, each in the product&rsquo;s native basis.

## The defensibility record

- [The Evidence a Jury Will See](/briefings/defense-counsel-briefing) &mdash; what a certificate, a lab report, and a chain of custody look like under challenge.
- [After Palmquist](/briefings/after-palmquist) &mdash; the litigation context that makes an uncovered metal a liability.
- [How HMTc compares](/compare) &mdash; the coverage claim, scoped and dated, so the comparison is defensible rather than promotional.
- [Two remediation pathways](/program/two-pathways) &mdash; what happens when a lot falls short, and the record it leaves.

## The citable editions

- [Program Manuals](/manuals) &mdash; the bound, DOI-bearing edition of each category. The edition named on a certificate is the one that governs it.
