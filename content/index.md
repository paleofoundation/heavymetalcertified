      <div style="width:80px;height:1px;background:#9f2727;margin-top:22px"></div>
    </div>
    <div style="min-width:0">
      <p style="font-size:16.5px;line-height:1.78;color:#3f3a3f;margin:0;max-width:72ch">Heavy Metal Tested &amp; Certified publishes numeric heavy-metal limits and certifies products against them. Limits are set per product subcategory, stated in each product&rsquo;s native basis, and default to the strictest applicable government maximum: tightened by a pooled distribution of peer-reviewed and government occurrence data for the four Tier-1 toxics and the analytes no government regulates. The program is operated and governed by the <a href="https://contaminantstandards.com" style="color:#1c43ad">Institute of Contaminant Standards (ICS)</a>. The peer-reviewed occurrence and toxicology evidence behind those limits is compiled openly at the <a href="https://heavymetalindex.com" style="color:#1c43ad">Heavy Metal Index</a>, a public reference to the published literature on heavy metals in food that ICS also maintains, so a reader can trace any limit back to its sources.</p>

      <h2 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:40px;line-height:1.15;letter-spacing:-.015em;color:#2b1f1f;margin:0">Common<br />questions</h2>

    <div style="min-width:0" class="hmtc-faq-list">
      <details class="hmtc-faq" data-hmi-faq="home"><summary><span class="hmtc-faq-sign"></span><span class="hmtc-faq-q">What products can be certified today?</span></summary><div class="hmtc-faq-a" data-hmi-faq-answer><p>Today 348 subcategory standards are in force as published pages, and 320 of them already carry at least one firm analyte limit. A firm limit is published for each analyte as it clears the five shipping gates, and the standard widens as the evidence base and governance review clear more cells. A product is eligible the moment a standard exists for its subcategory: a brand does not wait for the rest of its category.</p><a class="hmtc-faq-link" href="/standards">See the register &rarr;</a></div></details>
      <details class="hmtc-faq" data-hmi-faq="home"><summary><span class="hmtc-faq-sign"></span><span class="hmtc-faq-q">How much does certification cost, and how long does it take?</span></summary><div class="hmtc-faq-a" data-hmi-faq-answer><p>Fees are annual and scale with the applicant&rsquo;s gross annual revenue, so a small brand is not priced at an enterprise rate. Two-year terms take 10% off and three-year terms 20%. Standard processing runs four to eight weeks; rush handling is $5,000 for ten business days and $10,000 for three.</p></div></details>
      <details class="hmtc-faq" data-hmi-faq="home"><summary><span class="hmtc-faq-sign"></span><span class="hmtc-faq-q">What does the HMTc mark mean on a package?</span></summary><div class="hmtc-faq-a" data-hmi-faq-answer><p>That the product was tested at an accredited laboratory and found at or below every value in the published standard for its subcategory, on a ten-analyte panel, in the basis the product is sold in. The mark must appear with a scope line naming the standard and its edition, so the claim can be checked rather than trusted.</p></div></details>
      <details class="hmtc-faq" data-hmi-faq="home"><summary><span class="hmtc-faq-sign"></span><span class="hmtc-faq-q">Does certification mean a product is safe?</span></summary><div class="hmtc-faq-a" data-hmi-faq-answer><p>No, and the program does not claim it. Certification is a statement about a measurement against a published threshold, not a health verdict on an individual product or diet. A program implying otherwise would be making a claim its evidence cannot support.</p></div></details>
      <details class="hmtc-faq" data-hmi-faq="home"><summary><span class="hmtc-faq-sign"></span><span class="hmtc-faq-q">Who sets the limits, and are they stricter than government limits?</span></summary><div class="hmtc-faq-a" data-hmi-faq-answer><p>Each limit defaults to the strictest applicable government maximum, in the product&rsquo;s native basis. For the four Tier-1 toxics and for the cells no government regulates, the program pools peer-reviewed and government occurrence data and takes the stricter of that pooled figure and the government maximum: sometimes stricter than a government limit, never looser.</p><a class="hmtc-faq-link" href="/program/structure">How limits are derived &rarr;</a></div></details>
      <details class="hmtc-faq" data-hmi-faq="home"><summary><span class="hmtc-faq-sign"></span><span class="hmtc-faq-q">What is the difference between HMTc and the Heavy Metal Index?</span></summary><div class="hmtc-faq-a" data-hmi-faq-answer><p>The <a href="https://heavymetalindex.com">Index</a> is a public reference for the published literature on heavy metals in food; HMTc is a certification program that reads from it, not the other way around.</p></div></details>
