      <h2 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:40px;line-height:1.15;letter-spacing:-.015em;color:#2b1f1f;margin:0">What this<br />certification is</h2>
      <div style="width:80px;height:1px;background:#9f2727;margin-top:22px"></div>
    </div>
    <div style="min-width:0">
      <p style="font-size:16.5px;line-height:1.78;color:#3f3a3f;margin:0 0 18px;max-width:72ch">Heavy Metal Tested &amp; Certified publishes numeric heavy-metal limits and certifies products against them. Limits are set per product subcategory, stated in each product&rsquo;s native basis, and default to the strictest applicable government maximum &mdash; tightened by a pooled distribution of peer-reviewed and government occurrence data for the four Tier-1 toxics and the analytes no government regulates. The program is operated and governed by the Institute of Contaminant Standards (ICS).</p>
      <p style="font-size:16.5px;line-height:1.78;color:#3f3a3f;margin:0 0 18px;max-width:72ch">Most food certifications ask whether a producer followed a process. HMTc asks a narrower question with a checkable answer: was this product tested, at an accredited laboratory, at or below a published number, on a stated analyte panel, in the form it is actually sold in. Every term in that sentence is a variable a reader can look up, and the limits are published before anything is certified against them &mdash; so a brand can read the threshold it will be held to before it applies or spends anything on testing.</p>
      <p style="font-size:16.5px;line-height:1.78;color:#3f3a3f;margin:0;max-width:72ch">It certifies; it does not reassure. The mark does not assert that a product is safe, that analytes outside the panel were controlled, or that a different form from the same brand was tested. Where a limit is set tighter than the applicable government floor or the pooled literature supports on its own, the <a href="/standards" style="color:#1c43ad">standard names that gap</a> rather than smoothing it.</p>
    </div>

      <h2 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:40px;line-height:1.15;letter-spacing:-.015em;color:#2b1f1f;margin:0">Common<br />questions</h2>
      <div style="width:80px;height:1px;background:#9f2727;margin-top:22px"></div>
    </div>
    <div style="min-width:0" class="hmtc-faq-list">
      <details class="hmtc-faq"><summary><span class="hmtc-faq-sign"></span><span class="hmtc-faq-q">What products can be certified today?</span></summary><div class="hmtc-faq-a"><p>Standards publish per subcategory, and a product becomes eligible to begin the moment the standard for its subcategory exists &mdash; a brand does not wait for the rest of its category. Beginning means entering Private Readiness and planning against a published number. That is not the same as a category being <em>open for certification</em>, which additionally requires the methods, laboratory route, decision rules, agreements, mark controls and surveillance to be operational. Today every published category is at Private Readiness, and none has yet been declared open for certification.</p><a class="hmtc-faq-link" href="/category-readiness">Where each category stands &rarr;</a></div></details>
      <details class="hmtc-faq"><summary><span class="hmtc-faq-sign"></span><span class="hmtc-faq-q">How much does certification cost, and how long does it take?</span></summary><div class="hmtc-faq-a"><p>Fees are annual and scale with the applicant&rsquo;s gross annual revenue, so a small brand is not priced at an enterprise rate. Two-year terms take 10% off and three-year terms 20%. Laboratory testing is separate: the analyte panel is an un-marked-up pass-through the brand pays directly to its accredited laboratory, not a line in the program fee. Standard processing runs four to eight weeks; rush handling is $5,000 for ten business days and $10,000 for three.</p></div></details>
      <details class="hmtc-faq"><summary><span class="hmtc-faq-sign"></span><span class="hmtc-faq-q">What does the HMTc mark mean on a package?</span></summary><div class="hmtc-faq-a"><p>That the product was tested at an accredited laboratory and found at or below every value in the published standard for its subcategory, on a ten-analyte panel, in the basis the product is sold in. The mark must appear with a scope line naming the standard and its edition, so the claim can be checked rather than trusted.</p></div></details>
      <details class="hmtc-faq"><summary><span class="hmtc-faq-sign"></span><span class="hmtc-faq-q">Does certification mean a product is safe?</span></summary><div class="hmtc-faq-a"><p>No, and the program does not claim it. Certification is a statement about a measurement against a published threshold, not a health verdict on an individual product or diet. A program implying otherwise would be making a claim its evidence cannot support.</p></div></details>
      <details class="hmtc-faq"><summary><span class="hmtc-faq-sign"></span><span class="hmtc-faq-q">Who sets the limits, and are they stricter than government limits?</span></summary><div class="hmtc-faq-a"><p>Each limit defaults to the strictest applicable government maximum, in the product&rsquo;s native basis. For the four Tier-1 toxics and for the cells no government regulates, the program pools peer-reviewed and government occurrence data and takes the stricter of that pooled figure and the government maximum &mdash; sometimes stricter than a government limit, never looser.</p><a class="hmtc-faq-link" href="/program/structure">How limits are derived &rarr;</a></div></details>
      <details class="hmtc-faq"><summary><span class="hmtc-faq-sign"></span><span class="hmtc-faq-q">What is the difference between HMTc and the Heavy Metal Index?</span></summary><div class="hmtc-faq-a"><p>The <a href="https://heavymetalindex.com">Index</a> is a public reference for the published literature on heavy metals in food. HMTc is a certification program that reads from it. The citation runs one way &mdash; HMTc cites the Index, and the Index does not endorse HMTc or any certified product.</p></div></details>
