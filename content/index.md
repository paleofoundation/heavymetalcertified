# Heavy Metal Tested & Certified

Heavy Metal Certified is the authority surface for the **Heavy Metal Tested & Certified (HMTc)** program: the published standards, the methodology that sets them, and the briefings that explain what they change for brands, retailers, and counsel.

The standard is set against the **complete** heavy-metals food-and-supply-chain literature. The evidence is reported independently at the [Heavy Metal Index](https://heavymetalindex.com); HMTc applies that evidence to set certification limits, and cites the index one way — the index never justifies the certifier. That separation is what lets a certificate survive a regulator's question, a retailer's compliance review, or a plaintiff's expert at trial.

## Start here

- **[The Standards](standards/)** — published per subcategory, in each product's native basis, the moment it is defensibly ready. The live completeness ledger shows exactly how far the standard reaches today.
- **[Program Structure](program/structure)** — how a limit is set: the two pathways (clean and dirty), the native-basis pooling, the regulatory cap, and the five gates every analyte clears before it publishes.
- **[Standards Briefings](briefings/)** — ten analytical briefings on what HMTc is, why four metals is no longer enough, what *Palmquist* changed, and the cost of staying uncertified.
- **[Articles](articles/)** — analysis grounded in the independent literature index.

## What makes this defensible

The program publishes its methodology, names the gap between what the literature strictly supports and where each limit is set, and ships standards as living, versioned, dated documents. It does not claim a product is "safe"; it certifies that a product was tested to a stated threshold, in a stated basis, on a stated analyte panel, with the scope disclosed alongside the mark.

The program is independently governed by **[The Institute for Contaminant Standards](https://contaminantstandards.com)**.
