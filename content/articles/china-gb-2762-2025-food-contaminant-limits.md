## Abstract

China's National Food Safety Standard GB 2762-2025 replaced GB 2762-2022 and, together with its earlier Amendment No. 1, took effect for foods placed on the Chinese market on 2 September 2026. Read against the preserved primary text, the standard makes exactly one heavy-metal change: dried meat products were removed from the application principle that derives a dried product's limit from the corresponding fresh-food limit by a dehydration or concentration factor. As a result, the general meat and meat-products maximum levels now apply to dried meat products directly, on an as-is basis. Those category values, lead at 0.3, cadmium at 0.1, total arsenic at 0.5, and total chromium at 1.0 mg/kg, are themselves unchanged; what changed is which rule governs dried meat. Every GB 2762 cell that Heavy Metal Index binds was re-verified cell by cell against the 2025 text and is numerically identical to GB 2762-2022, so the register rebinds to value-identical successor rows and no downstream recompilation is required. The certification consequence is limited and it is stated plainly here: no published HMTc threshold changes automatically, no tracked claim cell moves, and any legal ceiling tighter than a published cell would enter the existing governance process rather than an editorial rewrite.

## GB 2762-2025 Is the Live China Framework Since 2 September 2026

GB 2762 is China's horizontal contaminant standard: a single instrument that sets maximum levels for lead, cadmium, arsenic, mercury, tin, chromium, nickel and other contaminants across the whole food supply. The National Health Commission and the State Administration for Market Regulation issued GB 2762-2025 under Announcement No. 6 of 2025, and it took effect on 2 September 2026, superseding GB 2762-2022 and its Amendment No. 1 <sup class="cite"><a href="#ref-1">1</a></sup>. From that date it is the framework a certified product must satisfy to be placed on the Chinese market. A tracker that still described the standard as adopted-not-yet-effective after 2 September 2026 would be reporting a status the calendar has already overtaken, which is why the Heavy Metal Index event record was moved to in-force on verification against the official announcement chain and the preserved primary text <sup class="cite"><a href="#ref-3">3</a></sup>.

## The Only Heavy-Metal Change Is a Dried-Meat Application Rule

The foreword to GB 2762-2025 names four heavy-metal changes, and all four concern the same food group and the same mechanism. Dried meat products were removed from the application principle that converts a dried product's limit from the fresh-food limit by a dehydration or concentration factor. In the 2022 text that principle listed dried meat products alongside dried aquatic products and dried edible fungi; in the 2025 text dried meat products are gone from the list, leaving dried aquatic products and dried edible fungi. The consequence is that the general meat and meat-products maximum levels now apply to dried meat products directly, on an as-is basis: lead at 0.3, cadmium at 0.1, total arsenic at 0.5, and total chromium at 1.0 mg/kg <sup class="cite"><a href="#ref-3">3</a></sup>. The category numbers themselves did not move. What moved is the rule that decides how a dried meat result is compared to a limit. This is a real change for a laboratory releasing dried meat products into China, because the compliance value is now read directly from the category maximum rather than reconstructed through a moisture factor, but it is not a new contaminant number and it is not invented here. The exact rows and bases live in the neutral regulation record, not in this analysis.

## Every Tracked Maximum Level Is Numerically Unchanged

Beyond the dried-meat application rule, GB 2762-2025 leaves the heavy-metal cells that Heavy Metal Index binds untouched. Each bound cell was re-verified against the 2025 primary text and found identical in value to GB 2762-2022. The re-verified set spans total chromium in milk powder and in liquid milk, total chromium in cereals and in legumes, total arsenic in milk powder, inorganic arsenic in compound condiment, lead in foods for special medical purposes for people aged one to ten years, lead in nuts and seeds, in condiments excluding spices, and in spices, cadmium in legumes and in peanuts, and lead in soy milk <sup class="cite"><a href="#ref-3">3</a></sup>. Because every bound value is unchanged, the register supersedes the 2022 rows with value-identical 2025 successor rows and no numeric recompilation follows. Any occurrence percentile a generated page may cite from this literature is a descriptive statistic and carries no compliance weight; the binding number is the government maximum level, and here that number did not change.

## Species and Basis Still Travel With Every Value

GB 2762 does not regulate arsenic as one analyte, and GB 2762-2025 preserves that discipline. Some rows are written as total arsenic, such as the milk-powder row, while others are written as inorganic arsenic, such as the compound-condiment row. A total-arsenic result cannot establish compliance with an inorganic-arsenic maximum, and converting one into the other by assumption would manufacture a legal value that the laboratory did not measure. The same care applies to basis. The dried-meat change turns on an as-is versus converted-from-fresh distinction, and the milk rows separate powder from liquid. A compliance decision therefore begins by resolving the product into the correct GB 2762 row, analyte species, and basis before any number is compared. The Heavy Metal Index record keeps species and basis attached to each row rather than presenting a single blended limit.

## What Changes for Certification

For HMTc the practical answer is narrow, and it is worth saying without drama. No published HMTc threshold changes automatically because GB 2762-2025 is in force, and no tracked claim cell moves, because every bound GB 2762 value is numerically identical to its predecessor. The register simply rebinds to the value-identical 2025 successor rows. A downstream read-across that leaned on a GB 2762 value, such as the total-chromium anchor taken from the liquid-milk row, is unchanged and remains valid. The verified structured limits can still enter the ordinary comparison against compiled [standards](/standards), which may confirm that a published cell already sits at or below the relevant legal ceiling or may identify a cell for governance review. That review runs through the program's existing evidence and approval process and cites the corresponding regulation records; it is never an editorial rewrite triggered by this article. Nothing here tells a brand to retest a product whose governing number did not change, converts total arsenic into inorganic arsenic, or invents a maximum level the standard does not set.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does GB 2762-2025 change any heavy-metal maximum level HMTc relies on?

No. Every GB 2762 cell that Heavy Metal Index binds was re-verified against the 2025 text and is numerically identical to GB 2762-2022. The standard's heavy-metal change is a dried-meat application rule, not a new number.

### What actually changed for metals in GB 2762-2025?

Dried meat products were removed from the application principle that converts a dried product's limit from the fresh-food limit by a dehydration or concentration factor. The general meat-products maximum levels for lead, cadmium, total arsenic and total chromium now apply to them directly, and those category values are unchanged.

### Is total arsenic the same as inorganic arsenic under GB 2762?

No. GB 2762 sets inorganic-arsenic limits for some foods and total-arsenic limits for others. A total-arsenic result cannot be compared directly with an inorganic-arsenic maximum; the species and basis must be resolved first.

### Does GB 2762-2025 require any HMTc standard to change?

No. The verified structured limits enter the normal compiled-regulation comparison. Any published-standard revision still requires the existing evidence and governance process, and no threshold changes automatically.
