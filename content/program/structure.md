Heavy Metal Tested & Certified is a controlled sequence: define the product, identify the governing rule, review existing evidence, test the genuine gap, make a product-specific decision, control the claim, and maintain the record.

## Product scope comes first

The program does not certify an abstract brand or a broad category name. Scope identifies the product, form, native basis, intended consumer, formula or material split, production facilities, markets, and governing standard edition before formal samples are accepted.

Powder, ready-to-feed, dry-as-sold, as-consumed, and contact-product migration bases are not interchangeable. A certificate and mark authorization travel with one defined scope.

## Eight priority metals, ten analyte determinations

| Tier | Priority metals | Decision consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | Lead, cadmium, inorganic arsenic, mercury | No transitional variance. A confirmed exceedance above the applicable certification limit escalates under the status rules. |
| Tier 2 | Nickel, aluminium, tin, chromium | A bounded transitional route may be available only under the published Status B conditions. |

Arsenic and mercury require species-aware control, and chromium requires speciation when a credible hexavalent pathway or reflex trigger exists. Total arsenic cannot substitute for inorganic arsenic, total mercury cannot substitute for methylmercury when the latter governs, and total chromium does not erase a required Cr(VI) determination.

The panel therefore covers eight priority metals through ten analyte determinations: lead, cadmium, total arsenic, inorganic arsenic, total mercury, methylmercury, nickel, aluminium, tin, and chromium with required Cr(VI) reflex control.

## How limits are set

Every standard states the product's native basis, analyte species, units, governing edition, regulatory source, and rationale. Each numeric limit is the strictest applicable maximum set by a credible sovereign government, expressed in the product's native basis. Where the government rule splits by product form or formulation, HMTc adopts that split and cites it.

Where no government regulates the exact product-analyte cell, the standard either discloses a read-across from the nearest applicable sovereign value or records an n/m determination and its control method. The 8:1 powder-to-liquid conversion is used only within infant formula. No occurrence distribution, program result, or brand result selects the initial certification value.

The Heavy Metal Index remains useful for category context, surveillance priorities, and research gaps, but it carries no compliance weight. Full methodology and cell-level rationale belong in the versioned standards and manuals.

## Publication and commercial readiness are different gates

A technical page may exist before a category can accept a formal certification submission. Commercial opening additionally requires complete analyte limits, methods, qualified laboratory capacity, sampling, decision rules, agreements, cost treatment, data notice, mark controls, surveillance, verification, and governance.

The [Category Readiness map](/category-readiness) is authoritative for commercial availability. A technical standards page is not an invitation to submit samples or pay a certification fee.

## Baseline and evidence reuse

Formal certification normally requires at least three eligible production lots before a status is assigned. A single favorable lot certifies nothing.

Qualified existing laboratory evidence can receive prior-data credit only after record-level review of laboratory accreditation and scope, analyte and species, method, sensitivity, matrix, lot identity, recency, custody, reporting basis, uncertainty, and completeness. Evidence useful for planning may still be insufficient for a certification decision.

## The five product statuses

| Status | Meaning | Mark use |
|---|---|---|
| A — Certified, Full | Every governing analyte determination is at or below the applicable certification limit under the decision rule. | Permitted within approved product scope. |
| B — Certified, Transitional | Only eligible Tier 2 conditions fall within the bounded transitional range; no Tier 1 exceedance or hard stop exists; required corrective action and end date apply. | Permitted only with required transitional disclosure and current authorization. |
| C — Probation | A confirmed nonconformity, repeated transitional condition, systemic control failure, or unmet corrective-action condition requires probation controls. | Not permitted on new production; lot release follows the governing rule. |
| D — Suspension | A severe nonconformity, hard stop, or unresolved control failure suspends certification. | Not permitted. |
| E — Revocation | Certification is terminated under the revocation rule. | Not permitted. |

Status attaches to the product, not the brand. Borderline and confirmation rules are applied before a result is represented as final.

## Pre-certification remediation and post-certification surveillance

These are different systems and must not be blended.

| Situation | Correct route | Certificate consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A product has not been certified and readiness or baseline evidence shows a gap | Private Readiness or, when the manual conditions are met, Confidential Remediation | No certificate exists and no mark may be used. |
| A certified product produces a later warning, nonconformity, or change | Surveillance, confirmation, status control and CAPA | Consequences follow the published status, release, mark, verification, legal, and appeal rules. |

The current Confidential Remediation Track is a pre-certification entry and support pathway only. It does not create a hidden certified status and does not promise to preserve a certificate after a later surveillance event.

Confidential business treatment never delays a recall, overrides a reporting duty, defeats legal process, or prevents a regulator request.

## Surveillance and record control

After certification, the program maintains product and lot identity, sampling and custody, laboratory evidence, method and sensitivity, status, trend, change control, CAPA, mark authorization, verification, appeal, renewal, and transition history.

Surveillance intensity follows product status and risk. A favorable later lot does not erase an earlier result; each event remains tied to its date, standard edition, lot, and decision.

## Mark and public verification

Only an authorized Status A or B product may display the mark, and the claim must stay within the product, form, analyte panel, standard, edition, status, and period shown by the certificate.

Optional directory visibility is not the same as authoritative verification. A certificate or product code used with the mark must resolve to the product-specific verification record when lookup is active.

## Fees, decisions, corrections, and appeals

A fee pays for evaluation and program services. It does not purchase an outcome, change a threshold, convert an incomplete panel into a certificate, or prevent a required status action.

Decision records preserve the evidence, rule, reviewer action, rationale, correction history, and appeal. Public errors are corrected publicly when they affect a public record. Appeals follow the published route and do not silently rewrite the original audit history.

## Standard change control

The edition named in the decision and certificate governs that decision. A later edition does not silently rewrite a past lot. Substantive changes are versioned, explained, and carried through a published transition. Initial limits and regulatory-source changes remain traceable to a sovereign government maximum or disclosed read-across. Separately, the ALARA [Standards Ratchet](/program/ratchet) may lower a published limit from that anchor when the capped HMTc certified-lot pool meets the published two-cycle trigger. Surveillance, CAPA, and supplier and process controls continue between revisions.
