Earlier editions of the program described limit-setting through two occurrence pathways. Where no government set a maximum, or for a Tier-1 toxic where a clean market could beat the law, the program set the number from the certification-relevant occurrence distribution, and the choice between two calibrations was the consequential one.

A lower-burden subcategory, where good product was the norm and contamination the exception, was set to a value near P97 of its distribution, calibrated to pass roughly the cleanest 90% of the market. A higher-burden subcategory, where contamination was endemic to the soil, the commodity, or the process, was set to a value near P45, calibrated to pass roughly the cleaner 40% and put the rest on notice. A row with no within-pair partner defaulted to the clean calibration. The percentiles were levers chosen to hit those pass rates against real occurrence distributions, measured at 94% and 42% against FDA per-sample lot data.

## Why the framing changed

Presenting the method as a percentile selection made a policy calibration look like the headline of the standard, and invited the reading that limits were a novel computation rather than a floor anchored in law. Method v2.0 reordered the emphasis: the default is the strictest applicable government maximum, a lookup that cites sovereign law and carries most of the panel, and occurrence data is a bounded input used only where the law is silent or where a clean market beats it. No published value ever exceeds the lowest applicable government ceiling in its basis.

The occurrence distribution still informs those open cells, and the internal derivation still computes a value to place them. What changed is the presentation: the program leads with the law, states an occurrence-set value as a value rather than a percentile, and records the calibration here rather than on the pages a brand reads to find its limit.
