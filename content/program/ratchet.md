## Initial setting and later tightening are different decisions

HMTc uses a government-limit-only method to set the initial value for each product-analyte cell. The program identifies the strictest applicable maximum level published by a credible sovereign government, expresses it in the product's native basis, and cites the controlling instrument. Where the exact cell is unregulated, HMTc must disclose a sovereign read-across or an n/m control; it does not fill the gap from an occurrence distribution.

The Standards Ratchet begins only after publication. It is the program's endogenous ALARA mechanism: eligible HMTc certified-lot results can lower the current HMTc limit as participating products demonstrate sustained, broad-based improvement. It does not wait for a regulator to revise the sovereign value that supplied the starting anchor.

| Mechanism | Data | Clock | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial limit setting | Credible sovereign government maximum levels | At initial publication and regulatory-source review | Sets the starting anchor. |
| Standards Ratchet | Eligible HMTc Status A and Status B certified-lot results | Annual review after publication | May tighten the current HMTc limit below that anchor. |

Heavy Metal Index occurrence evidence remains contextual. It can inform category understanding and surveillance priorities, but it does not set the initial limit, enter MaxPoL, or supply the ratchet pool.

## How the ratchet fires

For a given analyte, both conditions below must hold for two consecutive annual reviews:

1. The 80th percentile (P80) of the combined Status A + Status B trigger pool is at or below 80% of the current HMTc limit.
2. At least 50% of SKUs in that trigger pool have qualifying lots at or below 60% of the current HMTc limit.

The first condition tests aggregate movement. The second tests breadth. Requiring both across two reviews protects against a temporary clean season or one participant pulling the result down.

### Pool controls

The trigger pool combines Status A and Status B results so a participant cannot keep strong data out of the calculation through status classification. No single licensee may contribute more than 25% of the SKUs in an analyte's pool. When a licensee exceeds the cap, excess SKUs are randomly excluded before the trigger is calculated.

## Where the new limit lands

When the trigger fires, the new limit is the greater of:

1. the actual P80 of certified Status A lot results for the analyte; or
2. 80% of the current HMTc limit.

The second term is the step cap. No ratchet event lowers a limit by more than 20%, even if the certified market has improved further. Residual improvement may support another ratchet only after the trigger is met again on the next eligible review cycle.

This is the program's precise meaning of tightening toward 80% achievability: Status A results supply the achievability candidate, while the combined Status A + Status B pool and breadth test determine whether improvement is broad and sustained enough to act.

## Transition and certificate treatment

The period from announcement to enforcement is 12–18 months.

- Existing Status A certifications are grandfathered through the transition.
- Status B and Status C SKUs re-evaluate at their next CAPA cycle against the more stringent of the old and new limits.
- Status D and Status E products receive no transition protection.
- A licensee that exits and re-enters within 24 months is evaluated against the standard in effect at re-entry.

Each ratchet event receives a versioned change record. A new edition does not silently rewrite the decision made on an earlier lot under the edition named in its certificate.

## Forecast and accountability

At every annual review, the Program Operator publishes the current trigger-pool P80, the current limit, the projected next-cycle limit, and expected announcement and enforcement timing. Forecasts are projections, not commitments; the operative decision remains the two-condition trigger at the annual review.

The annual transparency record compares earlier forecasts with actual outcomes. If a forecasted limit differs from the eventual limit by more than 25%, the projection method is reviewed.

## What ALARA means here

ALARA is not a discretionary slogan and it is not the retired percentile-selected initial-setting method. It operates in two linked ways: each product has continuous surveillance, CAPA, supplier, formulation, and process-improvement duties; and the program-wide Standards Ratchet converts sustained certified-market improvement into a tighter future limit by a rule announced in advance.

Government sets the starting anchor. HMTc's own eligible certified-lot findings determine whether the program has earned the next downward step.
