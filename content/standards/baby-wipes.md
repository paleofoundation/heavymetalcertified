<section id="method" class="hmtc-section"><h2 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:30px;line-height:1.2;color:#2b1f1f;border-bottom:1px solid #e8e1db;padding-bottom:10px">How limits are set</h2><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Category 2 is the child-scoped personal-care category (ages 0-5). Three category-specific rules govern every row and are stated here once.

The first rule is that a child row is a floor, never a discount. Category 13 (adult leave-on cosmetics) and Category 14 (adult rinse-off cosmetics) cover the same product forms for adults, and a Category 2 row is never looser than the Category 13 or 14 row for the same form. Where a sovereign instrument publishes a child-scoped tier, that tier is the applicable cell; where it publishes an adult-market loosening carve-out, Category 2 does not adopt it.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Two such carve-outs bind in practice. Germany's BVL 2017 raises lead from 2.0 to 5.0 mg/kg for make-up powder, rouge, eye shadow, eyeliner, kajal and theatrical, fan or carnival make-up, and raises arsenic from 0.5 to 2.5 mg/kg for theatrical, fan or carnival make-up; Korea's MFDS raises nickel from 10 to 30 µg/g for colour cosmetics and 35 µg/g for eye make-up. Both carve-outs exist because adult decorative cosmetics carry heavy pigment loads. Neither is a child determination. A children's face paint or play make-up is applied to a 0-5 child's face, transferred hand-to-mouth and licked; certifying it at five times the general cosmetic lead limit would be indefensible.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Those rows therefore read across the same instrument's general-cosmetic tier, which is stricter, and the refused carve-out is named in the cell so a reader can see exactly what was declined and why. This is over-compliance against a real sovereign number rather than an invented one, and Category 16 (Supplements, live) set the same precedent by applying the strictest child-protective limit across an entire category.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">The second rule is that the ingestion-adjacent oral forms take the swallowed-product tier. Germany's BVL 2017 is the only instrument in the register that publishes a separate, stricter toothpaste tier (lead 0.5 mg/kg against 2.0 mg/kg general), and it exists because toothpaste sits on the oral mucosa and is partly swallowed. Children's toothpaste binds it directly. Children's lip balm reads across it: a lip product on a 0-5 child is applied to the vermilion border, contacts the oral mucosa and is swallowed at a fraction at least comparable to toothpaste, and the swallowed-product tier is the nearest sovereign cell for that pathway. Category 15 (Feminine Care, live) established this read-across pattern by taking the BVL toothpaste tier onto mucosal-contact products.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Face paint, children's make-up and nail polish do not take the toothpaste tier: hand-to-mouth transfer from those forms is incidental contamination rather than the product's designed contact surface, and stretching an oral-mucosal tier onto skin-applied decorative cosmetics would be a distinction the instrument does not draw.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">The third rule is that the mineral splits are real but do not move the number. The taxonomy splits four pairs on mineral content: cornstarch versus talc baby powder, chemical versus mineral baby sunscreen, non-ZnO versus ZnO diaper cream, and plain versus mineral-bearing children's lip balm. The split is correct, because talc, zinc oxide, titanium dioxide, mica and iron oxides are the raw materials that carry the metal load, so the two halves of each pair have entirely different contamination-risk profiles and must be sampled and qualified differently. But the split does not produce two different sovereign numbers, and this standard does not manufacture one.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">The only mineral-specific tiers in the register are loosenings. The EAEU TR CU 009/2011 tier for perfumery-cosmetic products containing more than 1% natural plant or natural mineral raw material sets lead at 5.0 mg/kg, arsenic at 5.0 mg/kg and mercury at 1.0 mg/kg, and Korea's MFDS raises lead to 50 µg/g for powder products using clay as a raw material. Every one of those values is looser than the general cosmetic tier this standard binds, and none is adopted for a 0-5 product.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">The mineral rows therefore bind the same ceilings as their clean counterparts, which makes them harder to meet, which is the intended and honest outcome. What the mineral rows add is a raw-material qualification obligation carried in the row's scope note, not a different number.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">The register does not support a number for every cell. Five of the eleven panel cells have no sovereign finished-cosmetic maximum anywhere in the register, and they are published as controls rather than as invented ceilings: inorganic arsenic (no jurisdiction speciates arsenic in a cosmetic, so it is bounded by the binding total-arsenic ceiling), tin, aluminium, total chromium and hexavalent chromium. The only hexavalent-chromium number in the cosmetic instrument family is EU REACH entry 2020/2081 at 500 µg/kg, which is scoped to mixtures injected intradermally for tattooing and permanent make-up, a different exposure route, a different product class and a different risk basis, and it is not read across onto surface-applied children's cosmetics.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">The only total-chromium, tin and aluminium numbers are EU Toy Safety Directive 2009/48 and ASTM F963 element-migration limits, which are numerically incomparable to a total-content cosmetic limit. Control is the correct fail-safe for all five: testing is mandatory, the analytical method and the escalation rule are published, and no ceiling is fabricated.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">One register gap is worth recording. Sri Lanka SLS 187:2025 is the only child-scoped cosmetic instrument in the register (skin powder for children, talc-base and starch-base), but every one of its rows carries the status 'draft guidance; not for implementation' and is gated out by the compiler, so it anchors nothing here.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Its published values (lead 10 mg/kg, cadmium 3 mg/kg, arsenic 1.5 mg/kg, mercury 1 mg/kg) are in every case looser than the German BVL general-cosmetic tier this standard binds, so no protection is lost by its exclusion; it is recorded as a register gap for verified re-ingest, not as a missed stricter anchor. The US FDA draft cosmetic lead guidance (10 mg/kg) is gated for the same reason and is likewise looser.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">An evidence note dated 2 October 2026 brings this page current with the Heavy Metal Index record ingested after 26 September 2026. The antimony ceiling is unchanged: 30000 µg/kg, strictly less than 30 mg/kg total content in the finished product, from Commission Decision (EU) 2023/1809, Annex I criterion 7.3.8, Table 9. The legal instrument text has not changed. No new DOI is deposited. Occurrence figures do not set the cell.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Four datasets measure antimony in wipes, each on its own basis, and none is a candidate limit. Washington Publication 14-04-014 reports 40.6, 61.3, and 70.6 ppm in three screened baby-wipe components and 95.8 ppm in moist baby-wipes packaging. Choi, Song, and Kim 2016 print digest concentrations in µg/L, not a sheet total in mg/kg. The 2 April 2026 China annex preferred reads run from 15.02 to 450.45 µg/L. Göçer 2025 separates lotion, fiber, and packaging and prints instrument ppb, including solution antimony of 695.40 ppb, with no made-up final volume. A concentration is not a dose, and the Index ranks no brands.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Three impurity figures are on the record and are not selected. Health Canada guidance treats antimony above 5 ppm in a finished cosmetic as technically avoidable. It is guidance, and the indexed text does not name wipes. Korea's cosmetic safety standard sets antimony at or below 10 µg/g and gives no wet-wipe-specific figure for the metal. Germany's BVL 2017 orientation value is 0.5 mg/kg antimony for cosmetics in general. That monitoring set was decorative cosmetics, baby powder, and toothpaste, not wipes. Table 9 remains the selected basis because it is written on total content in the final product, and the cell discloses that the Ecolabel is voluntary.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">This is Method v2. Why Method v1 was retired is on <a href="/program/method" style="color:#1c43ad">the method page</a>.</p></section>
