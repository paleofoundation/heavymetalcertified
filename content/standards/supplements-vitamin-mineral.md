<section id="method" class="hmtc-section"><h2 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:30px;line-height:1.2;color:#2b1f1f;border-bottom:1px solid #e8e1db;padding-bottom:10px">How limits are set</h2><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">No government sets a heavy-metal maximum level for a dietary supplement that protects a child. The adult frameworks that exist &mdash; sovereign food-supplement limits (lead 1000&ndash;10000&thinsp;&micro;g/kg), USP &lt;2232&gt; elemental-contaminant PDEs computed for an adult consumer, California Proposition&nbsp;65 warning doses &mdash; are written for a 60&ndash;70&thinsp;kg adult. HMTc instead holds every supplement, adult- or child-labelled, to the strictest sovereign infant-and-child-food maximum level for each analyte, because children routinely consume adult supplements (gummies, protein powders, greens, melatonin drops) and body-weight-normalised exposure makes the child the binding safety case.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Every value on this page is therefore a child-protective read-across, disclosed as such: the anchor is a real sovereign infant-food limit, named in the derivation below, applied to supplements as a voluntary over-compliance. The laxer adult floor is disclosed on every analyte and never selects the number. Where no government sets an infant-and-child-food limit for an analyte &mdash; total and hexavalent chromium, methylmercury &mdash; no number is invented: the analyte is placed under mandatory control with reflex speciation.</p></section>
