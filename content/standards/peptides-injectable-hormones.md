<section id="method" class="hmtc-section"><h2 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:30px;line-height:1.2;color:#2b1f1f;border-bottom:1px solid #e8e1db;padding-bottom:10px">How limits are set</h2><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">These products sit outside the food and dietary-supplement frameworks, but they are elemental-impurity-governed as pharmaceuticals. ICH Q3D(R2), harmonized across the US, EU, and Japan and enforceable through USP &lt;232&gt;-&lt;233&gt;, sets permitted daily exposures for elemental impurities in drug products and, in Table A.2.2, the corresponding route-based concentration limits (Option 1, for daily intakes of 10 grams or less). HMTc adopts those Table A.2.2 concentrations as the binding ceiling for this class, applied by route: oral products (SARMs, orally-dosed research chemicals) take the oral limits; injectable, nasal, and sublingual products take the stricter parenteral limits. Aluminium, which ICH Q3D does not address, is bound by the FDA large-volume-parenteral limit (21 CFR 201.323) on injectables.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Every analyte is measured on every lot by validated ICP-MS with reflex inorganic-arsenic speciation. Inorganic arsenic and methylmercury are bounded by their parent totals, and hexavalent chromium by total chromium (ICH Q3D governs chromium as the total element). Certification here is heavy-metal safety only: HMTc does not endorse, approve, or vouch for the efficacy or the legality of these products; the mark speaks solely to an elemental-impurity burden held within the ICH Q3D ceiling on the tested lot.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">This is Method v2. Why Method v1 was retired is on <a href="/program/method" style="color:#1c43ad">the method page</a>.</p></section>
