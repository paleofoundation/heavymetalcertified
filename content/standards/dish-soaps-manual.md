<section id="method" style="margin-bottom:52px"><h2 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:30px;line-height:1.2;color:#2b1f1f;border-bottom:1px solid #e8e1db;padding-bottom:10px;margin:0 0 20px">How every number on this page is set</h2>
<p style="font-size:14px;line-height:1.7;color:#5a4141;margin:0 0 20px;max-width:84ch;padding:13px 16px;background:#fdf4f3;border-left:3px solid #9f2727"><span style="display:block;font-family:'IBM Plex Mono',monospace;font-size:10px;letter-spacing:.1em;text-transform:uppercase;color:#9f2727;margin-bottom:5px">What this standard covers</span>Manual dishwashing liquids and soaps that clean tableware and food-contact utensils. GB 14930.1 Type B (detergents for tableware and food-contact tools) directly governs the product class; total arsenic binds the Type B ceiling and Pb/Cd/tHg are bounded by the grouped heavy-metals ceiling. Nickel and chromium are measured in this class (zhang2010; ecetoc1992) and screened.</p>
<p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Dish soaps (manual dishwashing) is a household cleaning product that contacts food or food-contact articles, not a food. Household cleaning products are largely unregulated for heavy-metal content: the ingredient-disclosure and eco-label regimes that govern the sector (EPA Safer Choice, Green Seal GS-8, California SB 258, the New York Household Cleaner Disclosure Act) set no heavy-metal maximums. The one credible sovereign that regulates this product class directly is China&rsquo;s GB 14930.1-2022 (<a href="https://heavymetalindex.com/regulations/china-gb14930-1-detergent-arsenic-limits-2022" style="color:#1c43ad">National food safety standard &mdash; Detergents</a>). Its Type B ceiling (detergents for tableware and food-contact articles) sets total arsenic directly; its grouped heavy-metals-as-lead ceiling (1.0 mg/kg, the sum of lead, cadmium, mercury and other heavy metals expressed as lead) bounds lead, cadmium and mercury individually as a conservative per-species reading. Every number on this page is in the product&rsquo;s native basis: total heavy-metal content in the product as placed on the market. That is GB 14930.1&rsquo;s own basis and the only basis a rinse-off product can be tested in.</p>
<p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Where no sovereign limit exists but the metal is a real or plausible contaminant of the class, the standard publishes an analytical method and an escalation rule rather than an invented threshold; those cells are listed under Screened by method below. Inorganic arsenic and methylmercury are speciation forms with no separate limit; their total-element parents (total arsenic, total mercury) bound them, and they are recorded as not material rather than left blank.</p></section>

<section id="controls" style="margin-bottom:52px"><h2 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:30px;line-height:1.2;color:#2b1f1f;border-bottom:1px solid #e8e1db;padding-bottom:10px;margin:0 0 20px">Screened by method &mdash; no government limit</h2>
<p style="font-size:11.5px;line-height:1.55;color:#a9a1a1;max-width:84ch;margin:0 0 14px">No sovereign sets a heavy-metal maximum for these analytes in a household cleaning product, yet each is a real or plausible contaminant of the class. Rather than invent a number, the standard publishes the analytical method and the escalation rule for each: certification measures the analyte and escalates an out-of-band lot to ALARA review, not to a pass/fail against a fabricated threshold. This is an honest control, not a silent gap.</p>

## Source regulations

Every government regulation weighed to set this standard, in the total-content basis of the product as placed on the market. Household cleaning products are largely unregulated for heavy-metal content; where no sovereign limit exists, the analyte is screened by method or recorded as not material rather than assigned an invented number.
