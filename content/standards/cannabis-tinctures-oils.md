<section id="method" style="margin-bottom:52px"><h2 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:30px;line-height:1.2;color:#2b1f1f;border-bottom:1px solid #e8e1db;padding-bottom:10px;margin:0 0 20px">How every number on this page is set</h2><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Cannabis has no uniform federal contaminant limit. Its heavy-metal ceilings are the route-split action levels of the credible sovereign cannabis programs: California (DCC 2026), Colorado (2022), Massachusetts (2020), Maryland (2025), Nevada (2020), and Israel (IMC-GMP 152, 2016). For each analyte the HMTc limit is the strictest maximum any of these governments sets for the product's administration route. The route basis is never conflated: the strict inhaled arsenic and cadmium action levels are never applied to an edible or a topical, and a permissive topical lead level is never applied to inhaled flower. It is a lookup, not a computation; occurrence never selects a value.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Four analytes bind directly to a sovereign action level: lead, cadmium, total arsenic, and total mercury. Total chromium is recorded as a screening ceiling rather than a health limit: it is dominated by benign trivalent chromium while the health-relevant species is hexavalent chromium, so the total-chromium action level is non-protective as a ceiling and any quantifiable Cr(VI) fails certification. Nickel, tin, and aluminium, which no cannabis program regulates for content, are held under mandatory analytical control; inorganic arsenic, methylmercury, and hexavalent chromium are enforced by reflex speciation against their measured total. Certification is heavy-metal safety only and does not endorse the efficacy or the legality of these products.</p></section>
