<section id="method" class="hmtc-section"><h2 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:30px;line-height:1.2;color:#2b1f1f;border-bottom:1px solid #e8e1db;padding-bottom:10px">How limits are set</h2><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Category 2 covers personal-care products made for children aged 0-5. Three rules specific to the category govern every row, and they are stated here once.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">The first rule is that a child's row is a floor-plus, never a discount. Category 13 (adult leave-on cosmetics) and Category 14 (adult rinse-off cosmetics) cover the same product forms for adults, and a Category 2 row is never looser than the Category 13/14 row for the same form. Where a sovereign instrument publishes a tier scoped to children, that tier is the applicable cell. Where it publishes a loosening carve-out for the adult market, Category 2 does not adopt it.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Two such carve-outs bind in practice. Germany's BVL 2017 raises lead from 2.0 to 5.0 mg/kg for make-up powder, rouge, eye shadow, eyeliner, kajal and theatrical, fan or carnival make-up, and raises arsenic from 0.5 to 2.5 mg/kg for theatrical, fan or carnival make-up. Korea's MFDS raises nickel from 10 to 30 ug/g for colour cosmetics and 35 ug/g for eye make-up. Both carve-outs exist because adult decorative cosmetics carry heavy pigment loads, and neither is a determination about children.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">A children's face paint or play make-up is applied to a 0-5 child's face, transferred hand-to-mouth and licked, and certifying it at five times the general cosmetic lead limit would be indefensible. Those rows therefore read across the same instrument's general-cosmetic tier, which is stricter, and the refused carve-out is named in the cell so the reader can see exactly what was declined and why. This is over-compliance against a real sovereign number, not an invented one. Category 16 (Supplements, live) set the same precedent by applying the strictest child-protective limit across an entire category.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">The second rule is that the ingestion-adjacent oral forms take the swallowed-product tier. Germany's BVL 2017 is the only instrument in the register that publishes a separate, stricter tier for toothpaste (lead 0.5 mg/kg against 2.0 mg/kg general). That tier exists because toothpaste sits on the oral mucosa and is partly swallowed, and children's toothpaste binds it directly.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Children's lip balm reads across the same tier. A lip product on a 0-5 child is applied to the vermilion border, contacts the oral mucosa and is swallowed at a fraction at least comparable to toothpaste, so the swallowed-product tier is the nearest sovereign cell for that pathway. Category 15 (Feminine Care, live) established this read-across pattern by taking the BVL toothpaste tier onto mucosal-contact products.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Face paint, children's make-up and nail polish do not take the toothpaste tier. Hand-to-mouth transfer from those forms is incidental contamination, not the product's designed contact surface, and stretching an oral-mucosal tier onto skin-applied decorative cosmetics would draw a distinction the instrument does not draw.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">The third rule is that the mineral splits are real but do not move the number. The taxonomy splits four pairs on mineral content: cornstarch versus talc baby powder, chemical versus mineral baby sunscreen, non-ZnO versus ZnO diaper cream, and plain versus mineral-bearing children's lip balm. The split is correct. Talc, zinc oxide, titanium dioxide, mica and iron oxides are the raw materials that carry the metal load, so the two halves of each pair have entirely different contamination risk profiles and must be sampled and qualified differently. The split does not, however, produce two different sovereign numbers, and this standard does not manufacture one.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">The only mineral-specific tiers in the register are loosenings. The EAEU TR CU 009/2011 tier for perfumery-cosmetic products containing more than 1% natural plant or natural mineral raw material sets lead at 5.0 mg/kg, arsenic at 5.0 mg/kg and mercury at 1.0 mg/kg, and Korea's MFDS raises lead to 50 ug/g for powder products using clay as a raw material. Every one of those values is looser than the general cosmetic tier this standard binds, and none is adopted for a 0-5 product.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">The mineral rows therefore bind the same ceilings as their clean counterparts. That makes them harder to meet, which is the intended and honest outcome. What a mineral row adds is a raw-material qualification obligation, carried in the row's scope note, not a different number.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">The register does not support a number for every cell. Five of the eleven panel cells have no sovereign finished-cosmetic maximum anywhere in the register and are published as controls rather than as invented ceilings: inorganic arsenic (no jurisdiction speciates arsenic in a cosmetic; bounded by the binding total-arsenic ceiling), tin, aluminium, total chromium and hexavalent chromium.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">The only hexavalent-chromium number in the cosmetic instrument family is EU REACH entry 2020/2081 at 500 ug/kg. It is scoped to mixtures injected intradermally for tattooing and permanent make-up, which is a different exposure route, a different product class and a different risk basis, and it is not read across onto surface-applied children's cosmetics. The only total-chromium, tin and aluminium numbers are EU Toy Safety Directive 2009/48 and ASTM F963 element-migration limits, which are numerically incomparable to a total-content cosmetic limit. Control is the correct fail-safe for all five: testing is mandatory, the analytical method and the escalation rule are published, and no ceiling is fabricated.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">One gap in the register is recorded here. Sri Lanka SLS 187:2025 is the only cosmetic instrument in the register scoped to children (skin powder for children, talc-base and starch-base). Every one of its rows carries status 'draft guidance; not for implementation' and is gated out by the compiler, so it anchors nothing here.</p><p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Its published values (lead 10 mg/kg, cadmium 3 mg/kg, arsenic 1.5 mg/kg, mercury 1 mg/kg) are in every case looser than the German BVL general-cosmetic tier this standard binds, so no protection is lost by its exclusion. It is recorded as a register gap for verified re-ingest, not as a missed stricter anchor. The US FDA draft cosmetic lead guidance (10 mg/kg) is gated for the same reason and is likewise looser.</p></section>
