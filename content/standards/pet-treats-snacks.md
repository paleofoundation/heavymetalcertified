<section id="method" style="margin-bottom:52px"><h2 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:30px;line-height:1.2;color:#2b1f1f;border-bottom:1px solid #e8e1db;padding-bottom:10px;margin:0 0 20px">How every number on this page is set</h2>
<p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Pet food is legally governed by <em>feed</em> law &mdash; the EU&rsquo;s Directive 2002/32 on undesirable substances in animal feed, the US FDA Center for Veterinary Medicine&rsquo;s guidance, and the AAFCO model regulations, with enforced pet-food maxima in Japan and draft maxima in China. Those feed limits are generally far laxer than the limits the same governments set for human food. Heavy Metal Tested &amp; Certified does not certify to the feed limit. For each analyte, the standard is the strictest maximum level a credible government sets for the nearest <em>human</em> food, adopted voluntarily as a human-grade over-compliance and stated in Pet treats / snacks&rsquo;s own as sold basis.</p>
<p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">This is an intentional over-compliance, and it is disclosed as one. Every row below shows the feed-law floor the product is legally allowed to meet, and the tighter human-food limit HMTc certifies to instead. That honesty is the point: a certified pet food is held to the maximum levels that govern food for people, not the looser levels that govern feed for animals. Where the nearest human food is itself unregulated for an analyte, the standard does not invent a number: the analyte is a mandatory-tested control, or a registered, evidence-tested not-material determination.</p>
<p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 16px;max-width:84ch">Basis is never conflated. A dry product (kibble, semi-moist, freeze-dried, treats, chews) reads across only from dry human foods as sold; a wet product (canned, raw, broths, milk replacers, fish diets) only from human foods as consumed. No wet-to-dry conversion is performed, and where a human analog is published on a more concentrated basis than the pet product the adopted value is conservative, never loosened.</p></section>

## Source instruments

Two legal registers set this page. The human-food maximum levels below are the standards HMTc certifies to; the strictest for the nearest analog binds each analyte. The feed-law floor is disclosed for contrast and never sets a number.

Human-food maximum levels (the certification basis):

| # | Instrument | Type | Role in this standard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | European Union human-food maximum levels | Maximum levels | Binds Lead, Cadmium, Nickel |
| 2 | Israel human-food maximum levels | Maximum levels | Binds Inorganic arsenic, Mercury, total |
| 3 | Paraguay human-food maximum levels | Maximum levels | Binds Total arsenic |

Feed-law floor (disclosed, not applied): EU Directive 2002/32/EC on undesirable substances in animal feed (complete and compound pet feed); Japan FAMIC pet-food standard (2021); China draft pet-food guidance. Every value weighed against these is shown in the human-grade table above.

The literature baseline for pet-food occurrence and for each analyte is maintained independently at the [Heavy Metal Index](https://heavymetalindex.com). This certification standard applies those findings; the two are kept editorially separate by design.
