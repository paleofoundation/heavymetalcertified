## Abstract

<p style="font-size:16.5px;line-height:1.78;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 18px;max-width:72ch">Version 2 of the Heavy Metal Tested &amp; Certified standard for baby wipes adds antimony (Sb) to the published panel. The numeric limit is PENDING CEO DECISION. No figure in this briefing is the certification ceiling. Measurements opened for this revision are summarized below. They are occurrence results, a derived sheet estimate, and one artificial-sweat simulant. Verified government limits are presented as candidate bases, and no choice among them has been made [1][2][9][10][11]. Total-content limits and migration limits measure different things.</p><p style="font-size:16.5px;line-height:1.78;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 18px;max-width:72ch">Version 2 adds Sb. No products had been certified under the prior version. The prior DOI stays live and is linked as the previous version: <a href="https://doi.org/10.83138/hmtc.std.2.baby-wipes">10.83138/hmtc.std.2.baby-wipes</a>, version 1.0 (2026-08-17) [3]. No new DOI is deposited for version 2.</p>

## Version note

Version 2 adds Sb. No products had been certified under the prior version. The prior DOI stays live and is the previous version, not a withdrawn one. It remains resolvable at [https://doi.org/10.83138/hmtc.std.2.baby-wipes](https://doi.org/10.83138/hmtc.std.2.baby-wipes) [3]. The current text of the standard is the [baby wipes standard](/standards/baby-wipes). Version 2 does not replace that DOI with a new one.

The antimony cell on that page is marked PENDING CEO DECISION. It is listed so the addition is visible, and it is excluded from pass/fail until a numeric limit is set. A lot is not passed or failed on antimony while the cell remains pending.

## What the measurements show

The limit is PENDING CEO DECISION. The figures in this section are the evidence summary. None of them is written into the certification cell.

Washington State Department of Ecology Publication 14-04-014, revised June 2021, measured total elemental antimony in baby-wipe components and in moist baby-wipes packaging [5]. Components were prioritized by X-ray fluorescence and confirmed by ICP-MS. The design is an upper-tail screen of products bought for that study, not a random sample of the category. The analyte is total elemental antimony. The report does not speciate Sb(III) and Sb(V). These results are occurrence. Occurrence does not set a live certification cell.

Choi, Song, and Kim 2016 digested approximately 0.2 g and diluted with 25 mL of water, not made up to 25 mL [6]. Table 5 prints an antimony range for wet-wipe nonwovens. The paper prints neither sheet figure in mg/kg. The conversion to a sheet estimate is uncertain, because the methods do not state a made-up final volume. Treating the final volume as 25 mL gives µg/L × 0.125, from mg/kg ≈ (µg/L × 0.025 L / 0.0002 kg) / 1,000, or about 23.48 to 2,444.79 mg/kg. The division by 1,000 is required because the quotient of those inputs is micrograms per kilogram. Treating the digest as 8 mL of acid plus 25 mL of water gives µg/L × 0.165, and a higher top of about 3,227.13 mg/kg. The paper prints neither figure. Neither reading is a candidate limit. The authors' attribution of the result to antimony trioxide or antimony glycolate catalyst is their inference. The highest sheet in Table 5 is labeled rayon only, and several polyester-labeled sheets were not detected, so the polyester-catalyst explanation is a hypothesis for wipes, not a finding.

The China Association for Quality Promotion of Consumer Products Safety page dated 2 April 2026 reports a detection count for total antimony in online baby wipes, and the annex results table prints the detected row in µg/L [7]. That unit is the one printed on the table. It is not a sheet concentration in milligrams per kilogram, and it is not a candidate limit. Brands named on that page are not repeated here.

Biver, Turner, and Filella 2021 measured artificial-sweat release from polyester textiles, not from wipes [8]. The extract row is a simulant result under ISO 105-E04. It is not absorption, and it is not a wipe-sheet total.

## What is being added, and what is not

Antimony is added as an analyte on the baby-wipes standard. The cell does not carry a certification number. The limit is PENDING CEO DECISION.

The standard's native basis for the cells already published is total content of the impregnating cosmetic liquid, in ug/kg of that liquid as sold. The nonwoven sheet is the applicator. A drinking-water concentration is orientation only. This briefing does not convert any candidate into ug/kg, because a conversion would be a choice, and no choice has been made.

## Candidate bases, with no choice made

The figures below are verified government limits, printed as the official texts state them. They are candidate bases. No choice among them has been made. None is written into the certification cell. The limit stays PENDING CEO DECISION.

Total-content limits and migration limits measure different things. A total in the final product, a total in polyester fibre, a migration from a topsheet or backsheet, and an extractable result in acid sweat are not interchangeable. The drinking-water figures are orientation only.

| Candidate basis | Figure, as printed | Status |
|---|---|---|
| EU Ecolabel absorbent hygiene products, Decision (EU) 2023/1809, Annex I criterion 7.3.8 | less than 30 mg/kg total content in the final product | Candidate. Voluntary. Not selected. |
| Korea MFDS Notice 2026-23 (adopted 2018), diapers | Sb 60 mg/kg or less by migration, topsheet and backsheet | Candidate. Not selected. |
| EU Ecolabel textiles 2014/350/EU, polyester fibre | 260 ppm total | Candidate. Not selected. |
| EU Ecolabel textiles 2014/350/EU, acid sweat (EN ISO 105-E04) | 30.0 mg/kg extractable | Candidate. Not selected. |
| United States, diapers, wipes, or textiles | no federal limit for antimony | Not a number. Not selected. |
| United States drinking-water MCL | 0.006 mg/L | Orientation only. Not selected. |
| EU Drinking Water Directive 2020/2184 | 10 ug/L | Orientation only. Not selected. |

Decision (EU) 2023/1809 sets less than 30 mg/kg as total content in the final product, and the criterion is voluntary [1]. Korea MFDS Notice 2026-23, adopted in 2018, sets Sb 60 mg/kg or less by migration for diaper topsheet and backsheet [2]. EU Ecolabel textiles 2014/350/EU sets 260 ppm total in polyester fibre, and 30.0 mg/kg extractable in acid sweat under EN ISO 105-E04 [9]. The United States has no federal limit for antimony in diapers, wipes or textiles. Its drinking-water MCL is 0.006 mg/L, orientation only [10]. EU Drinking Water Directive 2020/2184 sets 10 ug/L, orientation only [11].

Until a value is entered, the cell stays PENDING CEO DECISION and stays out of pass/fail.

## Where the decision sits

The decision is a standards decision on this side of the program. The measurements above are why antimony is on the panel. They are not the certification ceiling. The Heavy Metal Index remains the literature base and is cited one way; it does not set this ceiling [4]. This briefing names no brand. When a numeric limit is set, it will be written on the baby-wipes standard as a versioned change, with this briefing updated to say which candidate, or which other disclosed basis, was selected.
