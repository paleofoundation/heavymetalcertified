## Abstract

<p style="font-size:16.5px;line-height:1.78;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 18px;max-width:72ch">Version 2 of the Heavy Metal Tested &amp; Certified standard for baby wipes adds antimony (Sb) to the published panel. The numeric limit is PENDING CEO DECISION. No figure in this briefing is the certification ceiling. Measurements opened for this revision are summarized below. They are occurrence results, a derived sheet estimate, and one artificial-sweat simulant. Two candidate bases are presented, and no choice between them has been made: the EU Ecolabel criterion for absorbent hygiene products, Sb &lt;30 mg/kg, and the WHO drinking-water guideline of 20 ug/L, which is orientation only and is not transferable to a sheet total [1][2].</p><p style="font-size:16.5px;line-height:1.78;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 18px;max-width:72ch">Version 2 adds Sb. No products had been certified under the prior version. The prior DOI stays live and is linked as the previous version: <a href="https://doi.org/10.83138/hmtc.std.2.baby-wipes">10.83138/hmtc.std.2.baby-wipes</a>, version 1.0 (2026-08-17) [3]. No new DOI is deposited for version 2.</p>

## Version note

Version 2 adds Sb. No products had been certified under the prior version. The prior DOI stays live and is the previous version, not a withdrawn one. It remains resolvable at [https://doi.org/10.83138/hmtc.std.2.baby-wipes](https://doi.org/10.83138/hmtc.std.2.baby-wipes) [3]. The current text of the standard is the [baby wipes standard](/standards/baby-wipes). Version 2 does not replace that DOI with a new one.

The antimony cell on that page is marked PENDING CEO DECISION. It is listed so the addition is visible, and it is excluded from pass/fail until a numeric limit is set. A lot is not passed or failed on antimony while the cell remains pending.

## What the measurements show

The limit is PENDING CEO DECISION. The figures in this section are the evidence summary. None of them is written into the certification cell.

Washington State Department of Ecology Publication 14-04-014, revised June 2021, measured total elemental antimony in baby-wipe components and in moist baby-wipes packaging [5]. Components were prioritized by X-ray fluorescence and confirmed by ICP-MS. The design is an upper-tail screen of products bought for that study, not a random sample of the category. The analyte is total elemental antimony. The report does not speciate Sb(III) and Sb(V). These results are occurrence. Occurrence does not set a live certification cell.

Choi, Song, and Kim 2016 digested approximately 0.2 g and diluted with 25 mL [6]. Table 5 prints an antimony range for wet-wipe nonwovens. An approximate sheet figure is calculated here because the mass is printed as approximate. The formula is mg/kg ≈ (µg/L × 0.025 L / 0.0002 kg) / 1,000, which is µg/L × 0.125. The division by 1,000 is required because the quotient of those inputs is micrograms per kilogram. The milligrams-per-kilogram row below is that derived estimate, not a printed sheet concentration, and it is not a candidate limit. The authors' attribution of the result to antimony trioxide or antimony glycolate catalyst is their inference.

The China Association for Quality Promotion of Consumer Products Safety page dated 2 April 2026 reports a detection count for total antimony in online baby wipes, and the annex results table prints the detected row in µg/L [7]. That unit is the one printed on the table. It is not a sheet concentration in milligrams per kilogram, and it is not a candidate limit. Brands named on that page are not repeated here.

Biver, Turner, and Filella 2021 measured artificial-sweat release from polyester textiles, not from wipes [8]. The extract row is a simulant result under ISO 105-E04. It is not absorption, and it is not a wipe-sheet total.

## What is being added, and what is not

Antimony is added as an analyte on the baby-wipes standard. The cell does not carry a certification number. The limit is PENDING CEO DECISION.

The standard's native basis, unchanged by this addition, is total content of the impregnating cosmetic liquid, in ug/kg of that liquid as sold. The nonwoven sheet is the applicator. A drinking-water concentration, and a limit written for a different article, are not that basis. This briefing does not convert either candidate into ug/kg, because a conversion would be a choice, and no choice has been made.

## Candidate bases, with no choice made

The two candidates below are recorded so the decision has a public starting point. Neither is selected. Neither is written into the certification cell.

| Candidate basis | Figure, as stated | How it may be used |
|---|---|---|
| EU Ecolabel, absorbent hygiene products | Sb &lt;30 mg/kg | Candidate basis. Not selected. |
| WHO drinking-water guideline | 20 ug/L | Orientation only. Not transferable to a sheet total. Not selected. |

The EU Ecolabel figure is a content criterion for absorbent hygiene products, expressed as Sb &lt;30 mg/kg [1]. It is a candidate for the kind of article a wipe is adjacent to. It is not adopted here as the baby-wipes ceiling, and it is not restated in the standard's ug/kg unit.

The WHO figure is a drinking-water guideline of 20 ug/L [2]. It orients a reader to the concentration a health authority uses for water that is swallowed. It is not a sheet total, and it is not transferable to one. A wipe is not a litre of water, and the standard does not treat 20 ug/L as a mass of antimony in a sheet or in the impregnating liquid.

The certification limit, when it is set, will be a total-content value in ug/kg of the impregnating cosmetic liquid. Until that value is entered, the cell stays PENDING CEO DECISION and stays out of pass/fail.

## Where the decision sits

The decision is a standards decision on this side of the program. The measurements above are why antimony is on the panel. They are not the certification ceiling. The Heavy Metal Index remains the literature base and is cited one way; it does not set this ceiling [4]. This briefing names no brand. When a numeric limit is set, it will be written on the baby-wipes standard as a versioned change, with this briefing updated to say which candidate, or which other disclosed basis, was selected.
