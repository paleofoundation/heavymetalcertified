## Abstract

<p style="font-size:16.5px;line-height:1.78;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 18px;max-width:72ch"><em>This briefing is written for the two people who decide whether a heavy-metals certificate is worth its price: the Chief Financial Officer and the General Counsel. It makes one argument, in numbers you can re-run with your own inputs &mdash; that a Supreme Court decision handed down this winter quietly created a cost most brands in this category have not yet written on any spreadsheet, and that the cost lives in five places at once.</em></p>

<div style="border:1px solid #f2dcda;background:#fdf4f3;padding:24px 26px;margin-bottom:52px">
  <div style="display:flex;gap:12px;flex-wrap:wrap;align-items:center;margin-bottom:14px"><span style="font-family:'IBM Plex Mono',monospace;font-size:15px;color:#fff;background:#9f2727;border-radius:3px;padding:7px 13px">THE CLAIM</span><span style="font-family:'IBM Plex Mono',monospace;font-size:10px;letter-spacing:.12em;text-transform:uppercase;color:#9f2727">Technical report &middot; HMTc Standards Briefing 4 &middot; June 2026</span></div>
  <p style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-size:24px;line-height:1.45;color:#2b1f1f;margin:0">Before 24 February 2026, being uncertified to a heavy-metals standard was a marketing question. After that date it is a balance-sheet question &mdash; and the cost sits in five places at once, which is why almost no brand has written it down.</p>
</div>

## The five costs

<p style="font-size:16.5px;line-height:1.78;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 18px;max-width:72ch">The premise is the thing everyone in the category knows and would rather not say: the metals are already in the food. FDA&rsquo;s own Total Diet Study found lead in 15% of samples, arsenic in 43%, cadmium in 33% and mercury in 10%. The question was never whether a product carries a measurable metal &mdash; it almost certainly does, and so does a competitor&rsquo;s. The question is what a brand can show about it when someone with a subpoena asks.</p>
<p style="font-size:13.5px;line-height:1.7;color:#736b6b;margin:0 0 20px;max-width:84ch">Each component below is a separate model drawing on the same balance sheet. Magnitudes are illustrative anchors, not forecasts.</p>

<p style="font-size:11.5px;line-height:1.55;color:#a9a1a1;max-width:84ch;margin:14px 0 0">Components 4 and 5 are directional in the source briefing; no published magnitude is asserted for either.</p>
<h3 style="font-family:Lora,serif;font-weight:400;font-size:20px;line-height:1.3;color:#2b1f1f;margin:28px 0 12px">The component that sets the other four</h3>
<p style="font-size:16.5px;line-height:1.78;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 18px;max-width:72ch">The evidentiary asymmetry is the largest of the five. When a heavy-metal claim reaches a jury the plaintiff need not prove that one extraordinary lot poisoned one child; he has to tell a story in which the metals were present, the company knew or should have known, and the company did not do enough. &ldquo;They didn&rsquo;t test&rdquo; is a sentence a jury understands without an expert&rsquo;s help, and it was available in the baby-food litigation because the lead defendant had not tested its finished products for heavy metals until 2019.</p>
<p style="font-size:16.5px;line-height:1.78;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 18px;max-width:72ch">The defense against that story is evidence with three properties. It must be <strong style="font-weight:600">contemporaneous</strong>, generated routinely and before any complaint, so it cannot be dismissed as litigation-built. It must be <strong style="font-weight:600">independent</strong>, sampled and analyzed by parties who do not profit from a clean result, under ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation. And it must be <strong style="font-weight:600">against a published threshold</strong>, so that a result means something a third party can check.</p>

## Five models, one balance sheet

<p style="font-size:16.5px;line-height:1.78;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 18px;max-width:72ch">The cost of being uncertified does not resolve into a single figure, because it does not come from a single discipline. A chief financial officer who runs only the litigation model, or only the channel model, will undercount it badly. Underneath the five sits the figure that frames the whole question: a single public contamination event &mdash; recall, delisting or a serious lawsuit cluster &mdash; modelled at a one-year cost of $1.6m to $6.6m for a brand at $5m in revenue, $8m to $33m at $50m, and $57m to $187m or more at $500m.</p>

<p style="font-size:16.5px;line-height:1.78;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 18px;max-width:72ch">Set that against the cost of the thing that addresses all five at once. License fees are revenue-tiered and laboratory testing is an un-marked-up pass-through to an accredited lab, disciplined by the lab market rather than by the program. No single sticker price is published, because the fee scales with the brand. What is stated is the number the program has to stay under to pay for itself: for a mid-sized brand, on downside-risk avoidance alone &mdash; before a dollar of channel value and before any insurance credit &mdash; a break-even of about $112,500 a year, with all-in annual cost comfortably under $200,000.</p>

## What the number is not

<div style="border-left:3px solid #9f2727;background:#fdf4f3;padding:18px 22px;margin-bottom:20px">
<p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#5a4141;margin:0 0 14px">Certification does not make a brand safe, and a serious program will refuse to let a licensee say it does. The mark is a compliance signal under continuous surveillance, not a safety guarantee, and a credible program will not shield a licensee from a regulator. It does not eliminate contamination endemic to the food supply, and it does not turn a bad lot into a good one.</p>
<p style="font-size:15.5px;line-height:1.75;color:#5a4141;margin:0">What it changes is the evidence a brand holds when the contamination sitting in everyone&rsquo;s supply chain turns up in theirs. That is a narrower claim than &ldquo;safe&rdquo;, it is the honest one, and it is the only one worth a chief financial officer&rsquo;s signature.</p>
</div>
<p style="font-size:16.5px;line-height:1.78;color:#3f3a3a;margin:0 0 18px;max-width:72ch">One word in the argument does quiet work: <em style="font-style:italic">credible</em>. A certificate frozen at the four metals of the 2021 congressional report &mdash; arsenic, lead, cadmium, mercury &mdash; is silent on the nickel, aluminum, tin and hexavalent chromium that regulators from Brussels to Beijing to California already limit or list. A four-metal mark is therefore an uncovered exposure in its own right: a blind spot a brand pays for without being told it is there. That is why the standard underlying this analysis certifies against eight metals, expressed as ten analytes once the species that matter are split out.</p>
