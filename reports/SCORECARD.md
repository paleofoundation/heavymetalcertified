Why held — no disclosed `gov_floor` or `read_across` number sits on the
three empty claim-surface totals:

- **Sn** [`not_material`] — nearest sovereign is a disclosed wrong-basis companion; read-across rejected. Inorganic tin migrates from tinplate cans; chocolate, cocoa powder and chips are sold in wrappers/boxes/pouches, not tinplate cans, so there is no tin source or migration route. Tin is material only for tin-plated canned product.
- **Al** [`not_material`] — compiler not_material with value:null. Cocoa is a natural aluminium accumulator (aluminium is taken up from soil into the cocoa bean); the aluminium present is background, not contamination. No credible regulator sets an aluminium contaminant maximum level for cocoa or chocolate; dietary aluminium is managed by intake against the EFSA TWI (1 mg/kg bw/week) and JECFA PTWI (2 mg/kg bw/week), not by a per-food ML. Matches the existing chocolate/Al determination in standards-not-material.csv.
- **Cr** [`(missing)`] — compiler: no sovereign ML and no supportable read-across. No jurisdiction sets a total-chromium contaminant maximum level for any food (chromium(III) is an essential nutrient; only hexavalent chromium in drinking water is regulated). Cocoa takes up trace chromium from soil. Total chromium is screened as the substrate for the reflex Cr-VI speciation, but there is no sovereign number to align to.

What is needed before chocolate can mint:

2. Re-evaluate claim-surface COMPLETE (eight totals numeric; species ≤ parent).
3. House react-pdf regen of `content/static/standards/chocolate.pdf`.

claim-surface FAIL and **mint=HOLD**. Do not invent ppb. Do not write a DOI.
