# Part 1: Why this manual exists

The Heavy Metal Tested & Certified program for peptides, SARMs, and research chemicals is governed by five co-equal objectives. None may be sacrificed to advance another. The ruling is `docs/governance/hmtc-governing-principles.md`, the file named by the readiness manifest.

Limits do not derive from the Heavy Metal Index. Every published number traces to a named instrument or a disclosed read-across already on the standard record [1][2][3][4].

Certification is metal safety only. It does not certify efficacy, legality, or approval status.

## 1.1 Drive down contamination

The program exists to lower the heavy-metal burden in products that reach people. A mark that does not change what is in the vial, the powder, or the oral dose is not the work.

## 1.2 Protect consumers

Limits, lot testing, and the mark are instruments of consumer protection for one attribute: the metal content of the tested lot. A certificate that cannot be trusted does not protect anyone.

## 1.3 Protect brands who enter

A brand that enters in good faith, tests honestly, and stays inside the published rules is not set up to fail. Certification is offered only when the program has at least 90% confidence the brand can remain in the program.

## 1.4 Remain legally defensible

Claim-surface ceilings are disclosed read-across only. Seven metals are read across from the ICH Q3D elemental-impurity limits [1][2]. Aluminium uses the FDA injectable limit (25) on the injectable and nasal standards and the EU drinking-water limit (200) on raw powders and oral SARMs [3][4]. Species never replace the parent total. Nothing is invented.

## 1.5 Become the de facto standard

The program is written to be the instrument a laboratory, a brand, and a regulator can actually use for metal safety. It is not an approval, an efficacy claim, or a statement that the product is lawful to sell.
